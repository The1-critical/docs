---
title: "Skills"
---

Skills represent practiced competencies, things your character has learned to do rather than raw physical capability. Unlike jutsu, which are specific techniques with defined effects, skills are broad and contextual. How you use Stealth to move through a compound, whether by timing guards or using the terrain, doesn't matter mechanically. What matters is the rank you've earned in the skill.

Your skill cap is **5 + (total XP / 200)** ranks in any single skill. Raising a skill requires spending a Skill Point. You begin play with **10 Skill Points** and gain one additional point per 25 XP. Clanless ninja gain one per 20 XP.

Some skills are marked **Trained**, you cannot attempt them at all without at least five ranks. These represent knowledge rather than intuition.

All skill rolls are **1d20 + ranks**. Untrained, non-Trained skills may still be attempted at a flat 1d20.

#### **Athletics**

Movement, acrobatics, raw physical endurance, and precision under physical strain. Swimming against a current, vaulting over obstacles, landing a jump from a dangerous height, keeping your footing on unstable terrain, threading yourself between a field of traps. If your body is doing something demanding, Athletics is how well it does it.

#### **Awareness**

The ability to read your environment and notice what doesn't belong. A sound that's slightly wrong, the shadow moving in a window, the smell of someone's lunch on a supposedly empty roof. Awareness is your resistance to being surprised and your capacity to spot what others miss, including enemies who believe they're hidden from you.

#### **Chakra Control**

This is your ability to control the flow of the chakra within your own body, using it efficiently and shaping it precisely to do what you want. To a limited extent, it also represents how intuitively aware you are of the nature of chakra in general, and might allow you to notice when something's unusual about the surrounding chakra.

#### **Espionage**

The practical skillset of anyone who operates in the shadows. Picking locks, forging documents, palming objects without being seen, setting and disabling traps, reading body language, maintaining a cover identity, and generally making the world bend to your convenience without it knowing you were involved.

#### **Medicine _(Trained)_**

An understanding of the body,  how it fails, how it heals, and how to guide it toward the latter. At low ranks, field first aid and basic wound management. At high ranks, real surgical capability and a thorough understanding of biological systems. Cannot be attempted without training.

#### **Research _(Trained)_**

Structured inquiry, pattern recognition, and the ability to extract useful information from dense or obscure sources. Cipher analysis, library navigation, identifying historical precedent, decoding messages, and synthesizing what you know into something actionable. Cannot be attempted without training.

#### **Resistance**

Your body's innate durability against things that aren't weapons, extreme heat, intense cold, poison, disease, suffocation, and the various unpleasant status effects that combat tends to produce. Resistance is the roll you make when the world is trying to make you weaker, and you're refusing.

#### **Stealth**

The art of not being noticed. Moving without sound, blending into backgrounds, controlling your silhouette, staying downwind. Whether you're hunting a target or avoiding one, Stealth governs how well you remain invisible to the people around you, including the ones actively looking.

#### **Survival**

Operating effectively outside a village environment. Tracking targets across terrain, finding shelter and resources in hostile conditions, navigating without landmarks, identifying flora and fauna, and avoiding being tracked yourself. Survival is what keeps a long mission from becoming a fatal one before the actual dangerous part begins.

#### **Toxicology _(Trained)_**

Applied knowledge of poisons, their sources, their mechanisms, their timelines, and their countermeasures. Where Medicine might identify that a poison is affecting someone, Toxicology identifies which poison, where it came from, how long they have, and what will actually help. Equally useful for treating poisoning and causing it. Cannot be attempted without training.

<Note>
  **Summoning is not a technique. It is a Bond between two great beings; the arrangement is** you ask a guardian to come. And if you haven't earned the right, it won't answer.
</Note>

## The Old Pacts

The world has moved on. The forests have been logged. The rivers have been dammed. The mountains have been hollowed for ore. The great beasts that once roamed freely now live in the margins, remembered but rarely respected.

Yet the contracts remain. Written in blood. Sealed in chakra. Binding across generations.

## Rules of the Bond

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Breach of Contract" icon="shield-alert">
    <Warning>
      The bond is fragile. Break it, and the contract is sealed for **three weeks**, without exception.
    </Warning>

    The following actions will shatter a summoning contract:

    - Attacking your own summon directly
    - Catching them in an area-of-effect technique and wounding them deeply
    - Treating them with contempt instead of respect

    When a contract breaks, the summon leaves. Some summons have their own rules beyond these. They will tell you, if you listen. If you don't, you will learn the hard way.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The Avatars" icon="crown">
    The avatars are different. They have their own standards. Their own moods. Their own ideas about what counts as a favor versus an insult.

    <Tip>
      Sucking up is as bad as disrespecting. Hesitation is as bad as aggression. Respect is the only currency that matters.
    </Tip>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The Price" icon="coins">
    Summoning costs chakra. That much is obvious.

    But the real cost is harder to quantify.

    Every time you call a beast, you remind it of a world that no longer exists. You pull it from its home, its quiet corner of the world, and ask it to fight for a civilization that has forgotten its promises.

    The forests are smaller now. The rivers are dirtier. The old hunting grounds are crisscrossed with roads and power lines.

    They know. They remember. And they keep answering anyway.

    > The question is: when they stop, what will you have left?
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## The Reality

<Info>
  Most shinobi treat summoning as a convenience. A tool. A weapon in the scroll pouch. **They do not understand what they are holding.**
</Info>

The beasts remember when the Shinobi Confederacy was a dream in the minds of tired generals. They remember when the villages were just scared families in wooden walls. They remember the wars that didn't make it into the textbooks, the ones that happened in the forests they used to call home.

If you are lucky enough to earn their trust, you carry something more valuable than any jutsu. You carry a promise.

## Summons in Combat

<Info>
  Ninja will inevitably call upon their summoned allies in battle, whether against other shinobi or rival summons. The rules below govern how those allies behave, and what it costs to direct them.
</Info>

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Actions" icon="bolt">
    A summon's actions are tracked normally on the initiative count. However, summons **do not act on their own** while their summoner is present in combat. They must be directed, at an AP cost paid by the summoner. AP may also be spent to reduce a summon's action Speed.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Damage" icon="heart-crack">
    Summons have Vitality, not HP. When they reach 0 Vitality they vanish in a puff of smoke, automatically unsummoned. They **cannot be killed** in the field. Tracking them down afterward is a different matter entirely. Summons do not suffer wounds.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Fatigue" icon="gauge">
    Summons make Stamina and Chakra Exhaustion rolls normally. They do not have Fatigue levels, but when they fail a roll their penalties reset and they **automatically lose 20% of their maximum Vitality**. Overexertion and being beaten on have the same end result: they can no longer fight.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Unsummoning" icon="hand">
    A Speed 5 action dismisses any single summon, or multiple summoned creatures at once. If a summon is acting alone while its summoner is not in combat, it accumulates **5 AP every 20 IC** and acts freely within its listed behaviors.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### Directing Your Summon

<Steps>
  <Step title="Track the summon's initiative">
    Like any combatant, a summon's actions have a Speed. If it acts on IC 50 with a Speed 14 action, it is not free to act again until IC 64.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Spend AP to issue a command">
    When you want your summon to act, spend the listed AP from your own pool. The summon then takes the corresponding action. You may issue commands by speaking aloud or by the player choosing the creature's behavior directly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Optionally reduce action Speed">
    Additional AP can be spent to lower the Speed of the summon's action, paid by the summoner the same way as any other Speed reduction.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Summons do not accumulate AP on their own while their summoner is in combat, and they do not have Willpower. The summoner may not spend Willpower on a summon's behalf.
</Warning>

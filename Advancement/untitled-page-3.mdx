title: "Your Shotai"

#  

There was a time, not so long ago, when a shinobi's world was defined by the four-man cell. A jounin sensei, three genin students, and a handful of D-rank missions that taught them the weight of a kunai and the cost of failure. That system worked for a generation that grew up in the shadow of war, a generation that needed structure, hierarchy, and a clear path from the academy to the battlefield. But the world has changed mostly for the worse. The Shinobi Confederacy rose, and with it came a new kind of warfare. No longer was it enough to have a team that could complete a single objective. The conflicts of the modern era are larger, more complex, and more fragmented. A single squad cannot hold a border. A single team cannot gather intelligence, secure a perimeter, and eliminate a high-value target all at once. The great villages learned this lesson the hard way, through blood and failure and the quiet disappearance of shinobi who were simply outnumbered.

The solution was the Shotai. A Shotai is not a team. It is not a squad. It is something larger, something that bridges the gap between the intimacy of a three-man cell and the scale of a military company. A Shotai consists of three cells, each of three shinobi, operating under a single commander. Nine shinobi, bound together by training, trust, and the knowledge that they are part of something greater than themselves. Nine shinobi who know that when one falls, another steps up to fill the gap. Nine shinobi who understand they are soldiers but also a family forged in fire. The modern shinobi world is defined by the Shotai. It is the fundamental unit of operation, the building block upon which every mission is constructed. Whether you are a genin taking your first steps into the field or a jounin commanding a theater of operations, your Shotai is your anchor, your shield, and your sword.

Among the most influential of these shotais born from old world ideals was the legendary unit known simply as "The Balance", a Shotai whose structure and doctrine would become the template for the modern formation.

The Balance was not a traditional unit. It was formed in the aftermath of the Kazekage's assassination, when the Confederacy teetered on the edge of open war. Its commander was a jounin who had studied under one of the legendary figures of the previous era, a man who had learned that victory was not about power alone, but about the careful balance of force, defense, and precision.

---

## The Anatomy of a Shotai

The Shotai is composed of three cells, each with a specific role and purpose. The commander of the Shotai is typically a jounin or special jounin, though exceptional chuunin have been known to lead in times of crisis. The commander is responsible for the overall mission, the coordination of the cells, and the well-being of every shinobi under their command.

### Cell Alpha — The Sword

The first cell is the point of the spear. They are the ones who make contact, who engage the enemy, who draw the first blood. Alpha cell is typically composed of the Shotai's most aggressive and combat-capable shinobi. They are the ones who set the pace of the engagement and create the openings that the other cells exploit. They strike first, strike hard, and they do not stop until the enemy is broken or they are.

### Cell Beta — The Shield

The second cell is the anchor. They are the ones who hold the line, who secure the perimeter, who ensure that the enemy cannot escape or regroup. Beta cells are typically composed of shinobi with strong defensive capabilities, whether through ninjutsu, taijutsu, or sheer resilience. They are the ones who make sure that the Sword's work is not in vain. They absorb the enemy's counterattack, and hold the ground.

### Cell Gamma — The Shank

The third cell is the precision instrument. They are the ones who exploit the chaos created by the other two cells, who strike at the enemy's vulnerabilities, who complete the objectives that require finesse rather than force. Gamma cells are typically composed of shinobi with specialized skills: infiltration, assassination, intelligence gathering, or medical support. They are the ones who ensure that the mission is completed, even when the battle is lost. They move in the gaps, they strike where it hurts, and they never leave a job undone.

This structure is not rigid. A skilled commander knows when to adapt, when to shift shinobi between cells, when to merge cells or split them further. The Shotai is a living organism, constantly adjusting to the demands of the mission and the capabilities of its members.

## The Lifeline

The Shotai is more than just a military formation. It is a lifeline, a bond that transcends the ordinary relationships of the shinobi world. The members of a Shotai eat together, train together, and bleed together. They know each other's strengths and weaknesses, their fears and their ambitions. They are not just plain wartime colleagues; they are family. A shinobi who trusts their Shotai implicitly is a shinobi who can fight without hesitation, who can rely on their comrades to cover their blind spots, who can take risks that would be unthinkable alone. The Shotai is the foundation upon which victory is built. This is why the loss of a Shotai member is so devastating. It is not just the loss of a soldier; it is the loss of a piece of the unit's soul. The remaining members must learn to fight without them, to compensate for their absence, to fill the gap they left behind. Some Shotai never recover from such a loss. Others emerge stronger, forged in the crucible of grief. No Shotai operates at full strength all the time. Shinobi get sick. They get injured. They get called away on other missions. The modern shinobi world is too complex, too demanding, to allow for perfect attendance.

---

## The Substitute

This is where the substitute system comes into play. Every Shotai maintains a roster of substitute shinobi, individuals who have trained with the unit, who understand its rhythms and its dynamics, who can step in when a member is unavailable. These substitutes are not outsiders. They are part of the Shotai's extended family, shinobi who have earned the trust of the unit through their competence and their character. When a substitute joins a Shotai for a mission, they are seen as an extension of the team's doctrines. They become part of the unit, even if only temporarily. They must earn their place, prove their worth and if they do, they may be invited to join the Shotai permanently, becoming a full member of the family.

- When a member is absent from the roster, the players may choose a substitute from their Shotai's carefully maintained roster. Alternatively, a new character may be created with GM approval, representing someone recently brought into the fold.
- The substitute gains the cell bonus of the absent member they are replacing. If they step into Gamma cell for an infiltration, they gain access to Gamma's capabilities. They are equipped with the specialized knowledge the unit has accumulated in that role.
- But they also suffer a -2 penalty to coordination checks during their first mission. No matter how well they understand the Shotai's rhythms, no matter how much they have trained with these eight other shinobi, their first mission under fire is different. There are instincts they have not yet developed.

## The Shotai Legacy

The Shotai is a name. It is a reputation that precedes the nine into every room, every negotiation, every battlefield. As word spreads of completed missions and hard-won victories, as stories are told and retold in village bars and military command centers, the Shotai's reputation grows. Doors begin to open. Opportunities emerge that would not exist for an unknown unit. Resources flow more freely.

Reputation is measured on a scale from 0 to 100. It is not a static value but something that must be actively earned through the completion of missions. Each mission grants reputation points, with bonuses for particularly dangerous, high-profile, or impossible operations. A routine D-rank patrol earns token recognition. A successful assassination of a high-value target in enemy territory earns genuine renown. As reputation accumulates, the Shotai unlocks new benefits. These are not abstract numbers representing organizational morale. They are concrete advantages: better equipment, more favorable mission assignments, access to intelligence networks that previously would not have deigned to share information with an unknown unit.

---

### Stages of Recognition

**At reputation 20:** The Shotai's name has begun to circulate beyond the immediate military command structure. Village leadership recognizes the unit, though barely. This small increase in recognition translates to a \*1.2 modifier to requisition. Villages become more willing to equip the unit properly.

**At reputation 35:** The Shotai's reputation has grown enough that intelligence networks begin to notice. The unit gains access to a small informant network. When gathering intelligence in familiar territory, they gain +2 to Espionage rolls. The whispers in street markets, the bar talk of dock workers, the rumors floating through merchant caravans. All of this becomes accessible.

**At reputation 50:** At this threshold, the Shotai transcends the category of "capable unit" and enters the realm of "asset worth investing in." The village assigns a dedicated handler or support officer to the Shotai. This individual is the unit's liaison to command, their advocate when resources are being allocated. Once per 3 missions, the handler can provide intelligence on a known target, granting a narrative advantage in that scenario.

**At reputation 80:** The Shotai is now considered elite. The village provides better equipment without question. More favorable mission assignments flow to the unit rather than to competitors. The Shotai gains +1 to all rolls when operating in their home region, reflecting both superior resources and the home field advantage of operating among people who know and respect the unit's name as well as a free Luxury Dwelling as the team's main base of operations. The Shotai gains access to a training pool of talented young shinobi who can serve as substitutes, reducing the coordination penalty for substitutes to -1 (instead of -2).

**At reputation 100:** At this pinnacle of recognition, the Shotai's name is known throughout the entire Shinobi Confederacy. The unit can call in favors from other villages. They receive priority in all matters. They are the kind of unit that other shinobi look to for guidance and receive a Requisition modifier of \*1.6. Other units try to emulate their methods as they have all the benefits of the previous reputations.

## The Shotai's Role

The Shotai is the fundamental unit of shinobi operations. It is the formation that carries out the missions that define the Confederacy's power and influence. Whether it is a border patrol, an infiltration, an assassination, or a full-scale engagement, the Shotai is the tool that the villages use to achieve their objectives. The Shotai is also the unit that defines a shinobi's career. Promotion, recognition, and advancement all flow through the Shotai. A shinobi's reputation is built on their contributions to their unit, their ability to work with their comrades, their willingness to sacrifice for the mission. The Shotai is the lens through which the Confederacy views its shinobi. This is why the Shotai is so important. It is not just a military formation. It is the heart of the shinobi world, the unit that defines a shinobi's identity and purpose. It is the lifeline that keeps shinobi alive, the family that supports them, the structure that gives their lives meaning.

## The Problem of Scale

A Shotai is nine shinobi. That is enough to handle a mission, secure an objective, or hold a position. But a large-scale conflict cannot be fought by nine people. A border is not secured by nine people. A region is not pacified by nine people. When the Confederacy deploys forces to a contested area, they do not send one Shotai. They send many.

The question then becomes: how do you organize multiple Shotai operating in the same theater? How do you coordinate their efforts, allocate resources, and maintain cohesion across multiple units? The answer is a hierarchy of organization that mirrors the structure of military forces in the real world, adapted to the scale and needs of the shinobi world.

### Rensei (Platoon) — The Coordination Point

A Rensei is three Shotai operating together under a single commander. Typically, a Rensei consists of three Shotai, mirroring the structure of the Shotai itself: one Shotai takes the Sword role, one takes the Shield role, and one takes the Shank role.

The Rensei is the level at which operations are coordinated across multiple objectives. While a Shotai might handle a single infiltration, a Rensei can handle a compound assault: one Shotai secures the perimeter, one breaches the compound, and one eliminates the target. The Rensei commander coordinates these efforts, ensuring that the Shotai work together effectively.

A Rensei is formed when a mission requires more than one Shotai to accomplish. This might be a large-scale raid, a border patrol, or a complex operation requiring multiple simultaneous objectives. The Rensei commander is typically a jounin of significant experience who can coordinate the efforts of multiple units. The commander must balance the needs of the mission with the capabilities of their subordinate units. They must make decisions that affect the lives of dozens of shinobi. The Rensei is the level where commanders begin to think strategically rather than tactically.

### Chutai (Company) — The Regional Authority

A Chutai is three Rensei operating together under a single commander. This level of organization is typically reserved for major operations: border patrols, regional pacification, or large-scale assaults. The Chutai is the level at which a region is managed. While a Rensei might handle a single operation, a Chutai can handle multiple operations simultaneously, assigning different Rensei to different objectives. The Chutai commander oversees the entire theater, allocating resources and coordinating the efforts of subordinate units.

A Chutai is formed when a region requires sustained shinobi presence. This might be a contested border, a region with active insurgent activity, or an area where the Confederacy is projecting power. The Chutai commander is typically a jounin of exceptional reputation who is trusted to manage a region independently. The Chutai is where politics and strategy converge. The commander must navigate the complexities of local politics, manage relationships with civilian authorities, and of course balance the demands of multiple missions. They are the face of the Confederacy in their region.

### Daitai (Battalion) — The Theater Commander

A Daitai is three Chutai operating together under a single commander. This level of organization is reserved for major campaigns: the defense of a border, the pacification of a region, or the assault on a fortified position. The Daitai is the level at which a theater is managed. While a Chutai might handle a region, a Daitai can handle an entire front, coordinating multiple Chutai across a wide area. The Daitai commander is responsible for the overall strategy, deciding where to deploy forces and how to allocate resources. A Daitai is formed when a conflict escalates beyond the capacity of a single Chutai. This might be a major offensive, the defense of a critical position, or a campaign requiring sustained engagement over a large area. The Daitai commander is typically a jounin of legendary status or a kage's representative.

The Daitai is where wars are won and lost. The commander must make decisions that affect the lives of hundreds of shinobi and the fate of entire regions. They must balance the demands of their superiors with the needs of their subordinates. They are the architects of victory or defeat.

### Rengun (Regiment) — The Campaign Commander

A Rengun is three Daitai operating together under a single commander. This level of organization is reserved for the largest operations: the invasion of a territory, the defense of a village, or the execution of a major strategic objective. The Rengun is the level at which a war is fought. While a Daitai might manage a theater, a Rengun can manage an entire campaign, coordinating multiple Daitai across a wide area. The Rengun commander is responsible for the overall strategy of the campaign. A Rengun is formed when a conflict requires the full mobilization of a village's military forces. This might be a war between villages, a major invasion, or the defense of a village against overwhelming odds. The Rengun commander is typically a kage or a jounin of unparalleled status. The Rengun is where legends are made. The commander's decisions affect the lives of thousands of shinobi and the fate of entire villages. They must navigate the complex politics of the Confederacy, manage the expectations of their superiors, and inspire their troops to victory.

### Gundan (Division) — The Army Commander

A Gundan is three Rengun operating together under a single commander. This is the largest standard unit in the Confederacy's military structure, reserved for the most significant operations: the defense of a major village, the invasion of a territory, or the execution of a strategic objective. The Gundan is the level at which a major military operation is conducted. While a Rengun might handle a campaign, a Gundan can handle a major offensive or the defense of a major village.

**When It Exists:** A Gundan is formed when a conflict escalates to the point of existential threat. This might be a war between major powers, a crush event happening to a major village or their ally, or the defense of the Confederacy itself. The Gundan commander is typically a kage or a general of exceptional status. The Gundan is where the fate of nations is decided. The commander's decisions affect the lives of tens of thousands of shinobi and the survival of entire civilizations. They must navigate the complex politics of the Confederacy, manage the expectations of their superiors, and make decisions that will echo through history.

### Sogun (Army Group) — The Supreme Commander

A Sogun is three Gundan operating together under three commanders and the council of Kages. This level of organization is not standard; it is formed only when a truly existential threat requires the mobilization of all available force; it is rumored that the Fourth Shinobi war was the last time a Sogun was officially formed.

The Sogun is the level at which a war is won or lost. A Sogun is formed only when the survival of life itself is at stake. This might be a war against a foreign power, a rebellion threatening the Confederacy's existence, or a crisis that requires the coordination of multiple villages. The commander's decisions affect the lives of all shinobi and the survival of the entire Confederacy. They must navigate the complex politics of the Confederacy, manage the expectations of multiple villages, and make decisions that will define the future of the shinobi world.

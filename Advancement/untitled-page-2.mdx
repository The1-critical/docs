---
title: "Rank Benefits (Traveler)"
---

Rank benefits are specialized abilities acquired when one proves themselves among the lawless, the exiled, and the forgotten. Each rank offers several choices; you automatically gain any two of your choice, for free. These represent the different ways outcasts survive - some through violence, some through cunning, some through knowing when to disappear.

Some benefits are only available to those who belong to certain outlaw factions or who hail from particular territories of the broken Confederacy.

---

## Outcast (Chuunin Equivalent)

You have been cast out, or you left on your own terms. Either way, you no longer answer to any village. You have learned to survive on the margins - to read a bounty hunter's eyes, to sleep with a blade under your pillow, to know which back alleys lead to safety and which lead to a shallow grave. You are not a hero. You are someone who refuses to die.

### Eyes on the Margins

You have learned to read the room - the tension in a stranger's shoulders, the way guards shift their weight before they draw, the subtle signals that mean trouble is coming. When you are running with a crew (designated by the Game Master or agreed among players), you may activate this benefit up to XP/300 times per OOC day.

When you do so, you can call out a warning or instruction to one person (or use multiple activations to warn multiple people). They gain a +3 bonus to any relevant roll for following that instruction. If the instruction was to perform a specific action, that action also gains +3 Accuracy.

The instruction must be specific and actionable. "Look out" would not qualify. "Duck left" would. "Get him" would not qualify. "Grab his sword arm" would.

Generally speaking, a single instance of this benefit will benefit only a single roll or attack.

---

### Honour Code

Everyone on the run eventually finds something they will not leave behind. A promise made to a dead friend. A debt that can never be repaid. A line they will not cross, no matter how much coin is offered. You have a personal code - not a grand philosophy, just something that keeps you human when everything else wants you to become a monster. The Game Masters must approve your Code.

Once per two months (eight real-life days), while actively following your Code in a meaningful situation (refusing to abandon a wounded ally, keeping a promise to a stranger, taking a hit to protect someone who cannot protect themselves), you may activate this benefit. Doing so resets your Chakra Exhaustion and Stamina penalties to 0, and lowers your Fatigue by two categories.

However, if you ever knowingly act against your Code - even if it is the smart play, even if it means survival - you cannot use this benefit for three months (twelve real-life days).

---

### Ratel

You have been beaten, stabbed, left for dead, and you are still here. When your body says stop, something else says keep going. You can spend multiple points of Willpower (or activations of Limit Break, or effects that work similarly) on a single roll. There is no limit to how many times you may do so for one action, beyond how many Willpower you have to spend.

---

### Strike First

Some outcasts learn that the best way to survive a fight is to end it before it really begins. They favor wrapping their encounter up with a nice bow and moving on before the consequences catch up.

Whenever you spend a point of Willpower, you gain 6 Action Points. This benefit does not activate again until you gain Action Points from the Initiative Count reaching a multiple of 20. This does not affect other Action Point pools, such as those from the Eight Gates.

---

### Southern Swagger (Southern Roots Only)

The southern territories have a reputation for hospitality. What outsiders miss is that hospitality is a test. The ones who take advantage are marked. The ones who give it back become family. And family protects family. However for these upstarts, messing with one of them means you're messing with them all.

Whenever an ally within line of sight takes a Major or worse wound, or drops to 0 Hit Points, something wakes up in you. Starting immediately, and lasting for a number of Initiative Counts equal to your maximum Willpower multiplied by 5 after your next non-Interrupt action, you gain the following benefits: you ignore the first point of Accuracy penalty from Fatigue, you ignore the first point of penalty to defensive rolls from any single source, and any single-target Genjutsu affecting you ends immediately. You also gain a +2 bonus to defensive Genjutsu rolls during this time. Multiple instances of this ability overlap but do not stack.

---

### Boyz Haiku (Eastern Roots Only)

The eastern territories teach patience. Children there learn to read fog before they learn to read. Outcasts from the east carry that stillness with them - they do not flinch when the light goes out.

Every 15 Initiative Counts you spend suffering from any Visibility Penalty of any severity, that Visibility Penalty is reduced by 1 for you only, to a minimum of -2. This reduction persists even after you leave the effect that caused it, decaying at a rate of 1 per 10 Initiative Counts spent in normal lighting conditions. If you close your eyes or willingly blind yourself, this ability does not function. Temporary blindness from wounds or jutsu still triggers it. For area-of-effect visibility penalties, you must actually be inside the affected area to benefit.

---

### Tireless (Western Roots Only)

The western territories breed a different kind of stubbornness. When the sun beats down and the water runs out, you do not complain. You keep walking.

As a Speed 0 action, you may activate or deactivate this benefit. While active, you ignore the first point of Accuracy penalty from Fatigue and the first point of penalty to defensive rolls from any single status effect. (If a status effect applies a -2 penalty, you still suffer -1.) This specifically does not apply to any damage-dealing effects, such as Bleed, Ignite, and Poison. You may maintain this effect for a total of 50 Initiative Counts during a single battle.

---

## Renegade (Jounin Equivalent)

You have walked roads that do not appear on any map. You have seen the Confederacy's true face - economic collapse, civil atrocities, cold machinations. Your forehead protector, if you still have one, is a piece of stamped metal you keep as a reminder of what you left behind. Your loyalty is to the people beside you, not to any distant capital that stopped caring years ago.

### Weatherbeaten

You have been doing this outlaw thing for a while, and survival has become second nature to you by now. However, your techniques are rough and unpolished compared to village-trained shinobi. If you could reduce the Seal Speed of a ninjutsu you know to 0 using only your Dexterity and permanent Uniques, you may perform that jutsu without handseals, but its Chakra cost increases by 2. This additional cost is applied after all other reductions. This does not apply to Genjutsu, Fuuinjutsu, or Medical Ninjutsu.

---

### Rough Rider

All this walking has changed you, but the road takes as much as it gives. Your bonus to Chakra Exhaustion and Stamina rolls from Fatigue is raised by +3 per category (from +5 to +8, etc.), but your maximum Willpower is reduced by 1 (to a minimum of 1). This reduction is permanent and cannot be restored by any means.

---

### Lessons

You have picked up tricks from other outcasts, other wanderers, other people who have learned to survive through sheer stubbornness. Choose one Outcast rank benefit you do not already have. You gain it.

---

### Strike Last

When you have learned to end fights quickly, you also learn that sometimes the fight comes to you first. Your ability to counterattack has sharpened.

Whenever you spend a point of Willpower, you gain 8 Action Points instead of 6. This benefit does not activate again until you gain Action Points from the Initiative Count reaching a multiple of 20. Additionally, when you successfully defend against an attack, you may spend 1 Willpower to immediately make a single basic attack against the attacker as a Speed 0 Interrupt. This attack cannot be modified by any ability that increases its cost. This does not affect other Action Point pools, such as those from the Eight Gates.

---

### Parkour (Renegade Only)

You know every route that does not appear on official maps - the game trails, the drainage tunnels, the rooftops that connect across alleys, the sewer passages that bypass city gates. Your movement speed increases by 20% when moving through difficult terrain (rubble, undergrowth, crowded streets, rooftops). Additionally, when you take a Move action, you may ignore the first 5 yards of any immobilization penalty. This does not stack with other movement bonuses from the same source.

---

### Unbroken Chain (Renegade Only)

You carry the will of everyone who refused to kneel before you - the exiles, the fugitives, the ones who said "no" and kept saying it until they could not speak anymore. Once per battle, when you would be reduced to 0 Hit Points, you may instead remain at 1 Hit Point and gain a number of temporary Willpower points equal to your Resilience divided by 20 (minimum 1). These temporary Willpower points last until the end of the battle and cannot be spent on anything except defensive rolls and resisting status effects. This ability cannot be used if you already have temporary Willpower from any source.

---

### Empty Hands, Empty Mind (Renegade Only)

A full cup holds nothing new. You must empty yourself to receive more. As a Speed 5 action, you may discard one active Upkeep and one status effect of your choice. For the next 20 Initiative Counts, your Chakra Exhaustion and Stamina penalties are reduced by 2 each. You cannot activate a new Upkeep during this time. This ability can be used once per battle.

---

### Dirt Under Their Nails (Renegade Only)

You have walked the margins so long that you have become part of them. The powerful do not see you. The common folk do - and they do not forget a face that helped them. When you take the Hide action, your Stealth target number persists for an additional 10 Initiative Counts after you break stealth. You are no longer hidden, but anyone attempting to track you via Survival or Awareness rolls takes a -4 penalty. This penalty applies even while you are moving normally. Additionally, your folk hero reputation gives you +1 extra Infamy every time you gain Infamy from any source excluding experience thresholds.

---

## Straggler (Special Jounin Equivalent)

Special Jounin were the experts - the medics, the interrogators, the trackers. A Straggler is someone who has honed a single craft to a razor's edge, often because that craft is the only thing keeping them alive. They might be a surgeon who has treated more battlefield wounds than they can count, a tracker who has hunted defectors through three provinces, or a poisoner whose recipes have no antidote. Their expertise is their currency, and in a world without stable institutions, currency is the only thing that talks.

### Master of One

Choose a single skill which relates to your role as a Wanderer: Medicine for a field surgeon, Survival for a tracker, Chakra Control for a seal specialist, Toxicology for a poisoner, Research for an intelligence analyst, or Espionage for an infiltrator. You must already have the Specialization ability for that skill. When you roll below a 10 on the d20 when using that skill, you treat the roll as a 10, but only if you have not moved more than 5 yards on your previous turn (representing focused concentration). This does not apply if you spend a point of Willpower or use effects which follow the rules for Willpower. Your maximum rank in that skill is also increased by 3. You must have Game Master approval for which skill you take this for. You may take Master of One more than once, each time applied to a different skill.

---

### Sharpened Instincts

You have learned to trust your gut, but your instincts are sharpest in danger. When you spend a point of Willpower to reroll a skill check, you also add 5 points to the final result of the roll, but this bonus only applies if you are below half your maximum Hit Points or Vitality. You have one point of temporary Willpower, which does not count toward your maximum Willpower but is recovered normally (each out-of-character day). This temporary Willpower may only be spent to reroll skill rolls.

---

### Always Ready for Trouble

You take the concept of being prepared to its logical conclusion, but you work with what you can carry or steal. Whenever you purchase or scavenge non-consumable items (weapons, tools, equipment - not explosive tags, ammunition, or single-use items) using Savings or Scrap, you receive 50% more (rounded up). This does not apply to Requisition - outlaws rarely have access to formal supply lines. Spending 100 Ryo on lockpicks gets you 3 sets. Buying a katana? You also find a wakizashi in the same deal.

---

### Before the Fall

You have learned that falling is not the same as losing. Getting back up is what counts. Whenever you are wounded, you may make a Stamina roll. The target number for this roll is 5 plus (the wound's severity multiplied by 5), where Minor counts as 1, Major as 2, Severe as 3, and Critical as 4. Whether you succeed or fail on this roll, the wound or wounds you were just afflicted with (applying to all Wounds acquired on the same Initiative Count) have their penalties ignored for a number of Initiative Counts equal to your roll result (minimum 5). After that many Initiative Counts have passed, the penalties apply in full. Your roll result cannot exceed (10 + your Resilience divided by 8). If you succeed on the Stamina roll, you also reduce the wound's severity by one category after the delayed penalties apply.

---

### Crossroads Knowledge

You have picked up techniques and tricks from other traditions, other outcasts, other wanderers you have met on the road. Choose one Outcast or Renegade rank benefit from any territory (not just your own). You gain it. This does not allow you to choose territory-exclusive benefits from territories other than the one you originally came from. The Game Masters know which roads you have walked.

---

### Fading Ember (Wanderer Only)

You have learned to burn what you have left when there is nothing else to lose. When you are at Fatigue level 4 or higher, all of your attacks deal +10% damage and ignore 5 points of Damage Reduction. If you are at Fatigue level 5 (maximum), this bonus doubles to +20% damage and ignores 10 points of Damage Reduction. Using this ability does not prevent Fatigue from continuing to accumulate normally.

---

### Paid in Scars (Wanderer Only)

You do not care why you are fighting. You care that you get paid. This makes you unpredictable - your opponents cannot guess what you will do next because you do not even know until the coin is in hand. Once per battle, after an opponent declares an action but before they roll, you may declare that you are "recalculating." Choose one of the following effects: you gain +2 to your next defensive roll against that opponent, or you impose a -2 penalty to that opponent's next Accuracy roll against you. This represents your unpredictable, mercenary fighting style. You cannot use this ability if you have already acted on the same Initiative Count.

---

## Ghost (ANBU Equivalent)

ANBU were the shadows, the Kage's personal killers and spies. A Ghost is something else: a phantom with no permanent home, no recorded identity, and no loyalty to anyone who cannot pay. They carry out the dirty work that keeps the fragile peace intact—assassinations, intelligence theft, and the quiet removal of inconvenient people. They may work for the Regency, for the Crowns, for criminal organizations, or for private interests. Their mask is not a ceramic animal. It is whatever face they are wearing today, discarded as soon as the job is done.

### One Clean Kill

When you attack an opponent who is completely unaware of your presence - meaning you are in Stealth and they do not know you are hiding from them, which means this cannot be used if you hide from them in the middle of a battle - your attack has +4 Accuracy and deals +25% damage. However, if the attack does not kill or incapacitate the target, you suffer a -2 penalty to all defensive rolls for the next 10 Initiative Counts (your cover is blown and you are off-balance).

Additionally, you can prepare sneak attacks at twice the regular speed. When you abort a Prepare Sneak Attack action to perform a Sneak Attack, the duration of your Prepare Sneak Attack action only needs to be at least half the Speed of the action you are using as a Sneak Attack, rather than the full Speed.

---

### Unblinking Vigilance

It is impossible to catch you off guard, but your constant alertness wears on you. No matter what your Initiative roll at the start of a battle, your first action always occurs on Initiative Count 0. However, you begin the battle with 1 less Willpower (minimum 0) as the mental strain of perpetual readiness takes its toll. If multiple Ghosts with this ability act on Initiative Count 0, resolve simultaneous actions normally.

---

### Decisive Finale

You know the value of a single, decisive blow at the right moment. It ends battles - and lives. When attacking, if partial defense would apply to your attack's damage, you may spend 1 point of Willpower and cause all effects of partial defense to be ignored for that attack. The target takes full damage or avoids it entirely, with no middle ground.

This is declared before you roll damage. Your opponent may, in response, spend a point of Willpower of their own on their defense. If their reroll causes them to avoid the attack after all, your point of Willpower is not spent, and you gain 1 temporary Willpower (lost at end of battle). This represents the adrenaline of a near-miss.

This may only be used successfully once per battle. Remember, you can only spend 1 point of Willpower on a single action; thus, if you had already used a point of Willpower to reroll your Accuracy, you could not spend a second point for Decisive Finale on the same action.

---

### Less is More

You have learned to shed what you do not need - attachments, expectations, and even the weight of your own exhaustion. Whenever you advance to a new Fatigue level, you choose one status effect that is currently affecting you. That status effect is ignored for a number of Initiative Counts equal to your new Fatigue level multiplied by 10. If you use this ability on the same status effect again later, the duration resets to the new value rather than stacking. Fatigue gained from Clones, or Clones that were created while already suffering from Fatigue, do not gain the benefits of this ability.

---

### Blood on the Trail (Eastern Roots Only)

You have taken on the training of the east's famous hunter-nin, be it from a retired member, a jaded veteran, or through brutal self-teaching on the road. You may designate a single person as your quarry. Doing so requires an OOC day to study any available information on them - wanted posters, intelligence files, witness accounts - and to, through a mixture of not getting enough sleep and mild meditation, get yourself into the proper mindset for the hunt.

You have +6 to Survival rolls to track your quarry, and +5 to Awareness and Espionage rolls made against them. Your Accuracy for attacks made against them is increased by 3, and your damage is increased by 25% for the purpose of determining wounds. When fighting them, you have 1 point of temporary Willpower, and you gain another point of temporary Willpower every 50 Initiative Counts.

However, your obsession with the quarry makes you vulnerable to others. Against anyone other than your designated quarry, you suffer a -2 penalty to Awareness and defensive rolls. This penalty lasts until you end the hunt or catch your quarry.

It takes four OOC days of mentally detaching yourself from the hunt to end the quarry status of a person, whether because you have killed them, given up the hunt, or decided to let them go. You may only designate one person as your quarry at a time.

---

### Extensive Training

You have walked paths most outcasts do not even know exist. You have learned from ghosts who came before you, from abandoned scrolls, from the cruel lessons of survival. Choose one Outcast or Renegade rank benefit you do not already have. You gain it. If you choose a benefit that has a territory restriction (Southern/Eastern/Western Roots), you may ignore that restriction - the road has taught you to adapt.

---

### Friends in Low Places (Ghost Only)

You have garnered a reputation as not just a skilled killer, but also as someone who remembers the little people. While this does not come with many combat benefits, your dedication to helping out the small folk has not gone unnoticed.

Once per mission you may declare that you "know a guy" either in the current scene or somewhere off screen. This friend of yours should be established before major interactions are made, and is controlled by the Game Master. Examples include but are not limited to: a local blacksmith or merchant, one of the daimyo's guards, the local innkeeper, a dock worker, a courier, or any other individual who is not directly relevant to the main plot and is not of noble birth or otherwise of high social status.

This friend is willing to assist you in your current endeavors to the limits of their ability and station. They will not aid you in combat (they are, after all, just common folk) nor will they put themselves, their family, or their livelihood in danger. But they will otherwise do their best to help. The exact nature of that help is up to the Game Master to determine, but they are encouraged to ensure that the help is meaningful in some way.

Example assistance could be: asking a local blacksmith to repair or replace broken equipment for free, convincing a guard at a checkpoint to look the other way as you and your crew cross, or convincing a local tavern keeper to provide a free private room for the night and then "forgetting" to tell the local guards that you came by.

Additionally, the tales of your heroics and/or exploits, be they good or evil, have not gone unnoticed. As a folk hero among the masses, any time you gain Infamy from any source (excluding experience thresholds), you gain +1 additional Infamy. The powerful may fear you. The common folk sing songs about you - bad ones, with off-key choruses, but still.

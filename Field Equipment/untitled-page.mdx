#  

Weapons are the ultimate question every province and nation answers differently, a fundamental axis around which the balance of global power, control, and survival revolves. In the heartlands of the South, where the Kage's word is law and the Confederacy's bureaucrats count every blade, weapons are treated strictly as a privilege rather than a right. The average citizen in these territories cannot carry so much as a tanto without a permit that takes three weeks to process, requires two signatures to validate, and demands a fee that would feed a family for a month. The Northern territories maintain looser restrictions, and the Eastern islands are looser still. However, it is along the frontier borders of the Southern Province where the law runs thin, the gaps in governance are filled with violence, and the blades inevitably come out. This disparity creates a profound paradox: the same Confederacy that rigorously bans weapons from civilian hands maintains the largest arsenal in shinobi history. The system is explicitly designed for control rather than justice, sustained by knowing exactly who is armed and who is not.

## Slashing Weapons

### Trench Knives

_(Small, Slashing, Dual)_

Essentially knuckle dusters with the blunt front replaced by a serrated blade extending several inches past the fingers. They were first seen in the hands of Natsugakure's tunnel fighters during the Bone Market riots, where traditional swords proved too cumbersome in corridors that barely fit two men across. The serrated edge wasn't a design choice, it was a necessity born from the fact that straight blades kept getting stuck in the ribs of the dead, and no one had time to twist them free. Most are worn as a pair, though the old tunnel rats will tell you that one in each hand means both hands are committed, and committed hands are dead hands. They should know. Most of them are dead.

- **Cost:** 40 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 10
- **Damage:** 6d6
- **Speed:** 9
- **Special:** +1 Accuracy when using two of them to attack as a dual‑wield attack. You may perform handseals while holding trench knives, increasing the Seal Speed by 3 (whether you're wielding one or two of them).

---

### Wakizashi

_(Small, Slashing, Sword)_

The shorter companion to the katana, the wakizashi was once the mark of a samurai's honor. Now it's the weapon you reach for when the katana is too long, the tanto is too short, and you need something that fits in a messenger bag without drawing attention. The blade is about a foot and a half long, short enough to conceal under a coat, long enough to be useful. In the border towns between Fire and Wind, wakizashi are the most common weapon on the market; cheap, reliable, and nobody asks questions if you have one on your hip. The smiths of the Land of Iron stopped making them seventy years ago, which means the ones you find are either old, counterfeit, or both. They still cut just fine.

- **Cost:** 125 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 8
- **Damage:** 8d6
- **Accuracy:** +1
- **Speed:** 9
- **Special:** By taking a -1 to Accuracy, you can add the "Dual" tag to a Wakizashi if you use it with a Katana to attack.

---

### Tessen

_(Small, Slashing, Fan, Dual)_

A small war fan, used, much like their larger counterparts, to signal troops in combat. Or, if you're a ninja, to kill people with. The tessen, as used by ninja, is a handheld folding or non‑folding fan with, at the very least, a sharpened metal edge.

- **Cost:** 60 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 10
- **Damage:** 6d6
- **Accuracy:** +1
- **Speed:** 8
- **Special:** Tessen may be used to parry unarmed attacks.

---

### Katana

_(Slashing, Sword)_

The katana has survived the fall of empires, the rise of the Confederacy, and the obsolescence of the samurai. In the hands of a skilled wielder, it is still one of the most versatile weapons ever forged. In the hands of a shinobi, it's a tool that says something about its owner, that they respect tradition, that they have money to burn, or that they watched too many old war holos as a genin. The best katanas still come from the Land of Iron, but the Iron smiths have been making fewer each year. The demand for chakra‑conductive alloys has all but killed the traditional forge. Progress, they say. The Ironsmiths have a different word for it.

- **Cost:** 250 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 12
- **Damage:** 10d10
- **Speed:** 13
- **Special:** By taking a -1 to Accuracy, you can add the "Dual" tag to a Katana if you use it with a Wakizashi to attack.

---

### Nodachi

_(Large, Slashing, Sword, Two‑Handed)_

The nodachi is a statement. It says: I have the strength to carry this, the skill to wield it, and the patience to find someone who makes a blade this large. Every smith makes their nodachi differently, there is no standard, no blueprint, no consistency among these massive swords. Some are longer, some are heavier, some are balanced so poorly that the first swing is the last. The weapon is a relic, popular during the Warring States period, impractical in the age of motorized patrols and squad radios, and carried almost exclusively by shinobi who have something to prove. In the mountain villages of the Land of Earth, they still forge nodachi for ceremonial purposes. The ceremonies have not been held in thirty years. The forges are still hot.

- **Cost:** 200 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 14
- **Damage:** 11d12
- **Accuracy:** -1
- **Speed:** 15
- **Special:** Anyone attempting to parry a Nodachi has a -4 penalty; however, when attempting to parry using a Nodachi, your parry roll suffers a -1 penalty.

---

### Zanbato

_(Large, Slashing, Sword, Two‑Handed)_

The zanbato is what happens when blacksmiths stop asking "should we" and start asking "can we." It is a slab of steel with a handle, more siege engine than sword, more statement than weapon. Carrying one announces that you are either very strong, very foolish, or both. The weapon is nearly impossible to use effectively, the weight alone makes it unwieldy, and its sheer size means it cannot be drawn quickly or sheathed elegantly. Yet there are shinobi who have mastered it, who use its mass and momentum to devastating effect. They are rare. They are also terrifying. The rest just use it as a doorstop.

- **Cost:** 300 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 15
- **Damage:** 10d12
- **Accuracy:** -2
- **Speed:** 18
- **Special:** If you have 80 or higher DEX you may increase the damage by +6d12, by raising its Stamina cost to 20.

### Ono

_(Large, Slashing, Polearm, Two‑Handed)_

Farmers turned to axes when their harvests failed and the law could not feed them. In the frontier, the ono is as common as the plow, and twice as likely to draw blood. The blade is forged from scrap steel, broken swords, discarded plows, the detritus of war, and mounted on a haft of oil‑soaked wood. It is not elegant. It is not beautiful. It is a reminder that the world is running out of things that are not broken.

- **Cost:** 180 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 15
- **Damage:** 12d10
- **Accuracy:** -1
- **Speed:** 13
- **Special:** This weapon's damage is considered 20% higher for the purpose of inflicting wounds. When this weapon wounds, it automatically knocks the opponent prone.

---

### Naginata

_(Large, Slashing, Polearm, Two‑Handed)_

The naginata was once the weapon of the samurai's wives, used to defend the home while the men were at war. Then the men stopped coming home, and the women picked up the naginata and went to war themselves. The weapon is a pole with a curved blade on the end, simple, effective, and capable of cutting through armor and bone alike. In the modern age, it is a niche choice for shinobi who want to reach without sacrificing cutting power. The blade is forged separately from the shaft, which means it can be replaced if broken. The shaft is made of hardened wood, which means it will break before the blade does. This is by design.

- **Cost:** 150 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 16
- **Damage:** 6d12
- **Accuracy:** +1
- **Speed:** 14
- **Special:** Attacks from a Naginata do not trigger effects dependent upon being in melee with an enemy (such as most damage‑on‑hit effects).

## Piercing Weapons

### Kama

_(Piercing, Dual)_

Farming tools repurposed for killing. The kama is a wooden handle with a curved blade at a right angle, the same design used to harvest rice, now used to harvest lives. They are cheap, easy to replace, and versatile enough for both combat and utility tasks. In the Land of Water, they are still used for their original purpose. In the Land of Fire, they are also used for their original purpose, though the meaning has shifted. The blade is sharp enough to cut through wood, which means it is sharp enough to cut through you.

- **Cost:** 25 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 10
- **Damage:** 4d8
- **Accuracy:** +2
- **Speed:** 11

---

### Tanto

_(Small, Piercing, Sword)_

The tanto is a dagger, simple, direct, and effective. It is small enough to hide, sharp enough to kill, and cheap enough to replace. It is the backup weapon of every shinobi who has ever faced a situation where their primary weapon was insufficient, lost, or broken. The tanto's blade is double‑edged, which means it can be used for slashing as well as stabbing. Its length is short enough to be drawn in confined spaces. Its point is sharp enough to pierce armor. It is not a weapon for glory. It is a weapon for survival.

- **Cost:** 150 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 8
- **Accuracy:** +2
- **Damage:** 5d6
- **Speed:** 8
- **Special:** Ignores armor.

---

### Suna‑Ito

_(Small, Piercing, Sword)_

A blade so thin it can be hidden in a sleeve. The desert tribes of Wind perfected it for disputes that required discretion, the kind that end with a cough and a stumble. The steel is folded dozens of times, each fold aligning the grain until the blade is flexible enough to bend, sharp enough to pierce leather and flesh, and narrow enough to vanish against the skin. It is the weapon of the patient, the quiet, and the dead.

- **Cost:** 300 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 8
- **Damage:** 6d6
- **Speed:** 10
- **Special:** When making a Called Shot with this weapon, the Accuracy penalty is reduced to -1. This weapon cannot be used to parry Large weapons without breaking.

---

### Ninja‑to

_(Piercing, Sword)_

The ninja‑to is the weapon of practicality. Straight‑bladed, square‑guarded, and designed for quick draws and rapid strikes. Unlike the curved katana, the ninja‑to is straight because straight blades are easier to manufacture, easier to maintain, and easier to use in confined spaces. The handguard is square because square handguards can be used as steps for climbing, hooks for retrieving items, and, in desperate situations, makeshift grappling points. The weapon is not beautiful. It is not elegant. It is useful. In the end, that is what matters.

- **Cost:** 100 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 9
- **Damage:** 7d10
- **Speed:** 9
- **Special:** Drawing and Sheathing a ninja‑to is a Speed 2 action, both of which can be reduced by Quick Draw. Drawing and Sheathing a Ninja‑to can also be done alongside an interrupt, increasing the speed of that Interrupt by 2 (which is also reduced by Quick Draw).

### Yari

_(Large, Piercing, Polearm, Two‑Handed)_

The yari is the simplest weapon ever designed: a wooden pole, a straight blade, a sharp point. It has not changed in five hundred years because it does not need to. The yari is effective against infantry, cavalry, and, if thrown correctly, anything that is foolish enough to stand still. In the hands of a skilled user, it can be used to poke, slash, or parry. In the hands of an unskilled user, it is a stick with a point, which is still more effective than most weapons. The blade is made of steel. The shaft is made of wood. The design is practical.

- **Cost:** 125 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 14
- **Damage:** 10d12
- **Speed:** 14
- **Special:** This weapon's damage is considered 20% higher for the purpose of inflicting wounds. Attacks from a Yari do not trigger effects dependent upon being in melee with an enemy (such as most damage‑on‑hit effects).

---

### Shikomizue

_(Small, Piercing, Sword)_

A walking stick that is not a walking stick. The blade hides within the cane's hollow shaft, held in place by friction and the weight of the wielder's hand. When the moment comes, the handle pulls free and the steel slides out, silent, sudden, and sharp enough to pierce armor. In the frontier towns where weapons are banned and patrols are frequent, the shikomizue is the weapon of the patient, the wealthy, and the dead.

- **Cost:** 200 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 8
- **Damage:** 8d8
- **Accuracy:** +2
- **Speed:** 8
- **Special:** This weapon is considered concealed for the purposes of detection (granting a +5 bonus to Espionage rolls made to hide it). Drawing the Shikomizue is a Speed 0 action, but sheathing it requires Speed 6.

## Blunt Weapons

### Bo Staff

_(Blunt, Polearm, Two‑Handed)_

The bo staff is a hard wooden pole, six feet long and simple as can be. It has no edge, no point, no hidden mechanisms. It is a stick. It is also one of the most effective weapons ever created. The bo staff is fast, versatile, and difficult to disarm. It can be used to block, strike, trip, or throw. It can be spun, twirled, or simply swung. The people who wield it usually call it something else. The people who are hit by it usually don't remember what it was called at all.

- **Cost:** 100 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 14
- **Area:** 3
- **Damage:** 8d10
- **Accuracy:** +1
- **Speed:** 14
- **Special:** Attacks from a Bo Staff do not trigger effects dependent upon being in melee with an enemy (such as most damage‑on‑hit effects). You can use CQC Taijutsu solo techniques (but not Combo taijutsu or Grapple) even while wielding a Bo Staff, so long as you are not wielding any other weapons. When attacking, you may "dual wield" a Bo staff with itself by attacking with both ends, allowing it to be considered to have the 'Dual' tag for the attack, but when doing so you lose its Accuracy bonus. If used in this way, you do not receive the Two‑handed damage bonus multiplier.

---

### Gunbai

_(Large, Blunt, Fan, Two‑Handed)_

The gunbai is a relic. Originally used by generals to signal troops, it was adapted by ninja who recognized that a large fan could be just as deadly as a sword. The gunbai's metal slats are reinforced with chakra‑conductive alloys, allowing it to withstand blows that would shatter a lesser weapon. Its folding mechanism means it can be carried easily and deployed instantly. The generals who once wielded them are long dead. The shinobi who wield them now are no less dangerous.

- **Cost:** 200 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 12
- **Damage:** 8d12
- **Speed:** 12
- **Special:** Blocking weapons with a Gunbai reduces their damage by 50%, rather than 25%.

---

### Kusari‑gama

_(Blunt, Chain)_

The kusari‑gama is a weapon of extremes: a weighted chain on one end, a curved blade on the other. It is used to entangle, disarm, and strike from a distance. It is also used to strike the wielder, if they are not careful. The chain is heavy enough to break bones, the blade sharp enough to draw blood. Together, they are a weapon that requires skill, practice, and a willingness to accept that you will occasionally hit yourself. The weight is usually iron. The blade is usually steel. The bruises are usually your own.

- **Cost:** 125 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 12
- **Damage:** 8d10
- **Range:** 5
- **Speed:** 12
- **Special:** The stats above are for the weighted ball end, which is attached via chain to a Kama (which uses the standard Kama statistics). Using this weapon takes both hands, but does not grant the normal Two‑Handed bonus (or the tag). The weighted end can be used to perform Disarm or Grab (the E‑Rank Grapple jutsu) with its normal range, and grants a +3 to Grapple checks it's used with.

Grappling someone with this weapon does not impose a penalty on your own Dodge or Parry interrupts, though the ball end of the weapon can not be used for anything else while a grapple is maintained. Ignite statuses do not transfer between grapplers, and you must keep ahold of the weapon to maintain the grapple. The person you're grappling cannot move further away from you than this weapon's range: if they would do something that would move them further, you are moved as well (and vice‑versa), and either may attempt to restrain the other (as described under Grab). You can always elect to end the grapple by simply discarding the weapon (see the Discard weapon action), whether or not you are in control of the grapple. Once someone is grappled with this weapon, you can perform any Grapple jutsu of C or lower rank on them, with the exception of Headbutt, Suplex, and Pressure Point. Joint Lock uses the weapon's damage instead of unarmed attack damage. The weapon is considered to have unlimited limbs.

---

### Nunchaku

_(Blunt)_

Nunchaku are two wooden handles connected by a length of rope or chain. They are a weapon of rhythm and precision, swung in arcs that build momentum and deliver blows with surprising force. They are also a weapon of self‑loathing, used by genin who have not yet learned that practical weapons are more effective than cool ones. The veterans who watch them train rarely say anything. Everyone needs to learn that lesson for themselves. The bruises will teach it faster than any instructor.

- **Cost:** 80 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 8
- **Damage:** 5d8
- **Accuracy:** +1
- **Speed:** 10
- **Special:** Consecutive basic attacks (or dual wield attacks with itself, see below) with Nunchaku with no other non‑interrupt actions lowers its Speed by 1 and increases your Damage Bonus by 0.5, to a maximum of -4 and +2. The Speed reduction acts in all ways as if you'd spent AP to do so (and follows its normal limits). By using a single Nunchaku with both hands (which does not grant the Two‑handed tag), you can add the "Dual" tag to any attack you make with a Nunchaku, but you lose its Accuracy bonus for that attack.

---

### Sai

_(Blunt, Dual)_

The sai is a defensive weapon, a blunted spike with two prongs extending from the handle. It is designed to catch blades, trap weapons, and disarm opponents. It is not designed to kill, which makes it a favorite among shinobi who prefer to keep their targets alive for questioning. The sai's prongs are reinforced to withstand blows from swords. Its handle is wrapped in leather to absorb impact. Its user is usually calm, controlled, and carrying a second sai, because one is never enough.

- **Cost:** 75 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 8
- **Damage:** 4d8
- **Accuracy:** +1
- **Speed:** 9
- **Special:** +2 Parry. After you successfully parry any non‑Large weapon with your sai, you may elect to perform the Disarm action as an Interrupt against your opponent as a Stamina 12 action, using your parry roll as your attack roll for the disarm. If you successfully Disarm your opponent in this way, you can elect to catch their weapon in your other hand rather than send it flying, so long as you aren't holding anything in it already; if you wish to drop a weapon to catch theirs, you must use the Discard Weapon link alongside your Parry prior to the disarm attempt to free a hand.

---

### Tetsubo

_(Large, Blunt, Polearm, Two‑Handed)_

The tetsubo is a large wooden pole reinforced with iron studs. It is heavy, slow, and devastating. It is the weapon of choice for shinobi who want to demonstrate that they are stronger than their opponents. It is also the weapon of choice for shinobi who want to break their opponents' weapons, which is a more subtle way of proving the same point. The iron studs increase the damage and weight. The weight increases the momentum. The momentum breaks the bone.

- **Cost:** 250 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 18
- **Damage:** 15d12
- **Accuracy:** -3
- **Speed:** 16, Delay 8
- **Special:** Stun 6. Attacks from a Tetsubo do not trigger effects dependent upon being in melee with an enemy (such as most damage‑on‑hit effects). Anyone attempting to parry this weapon takes a -3 to their parry roll, and this weapon reduces blocking by an additional 25% (which means, normally, that blocking does not reduce its damage at all).

---

### Hachiman Tetsu

_(Large, Blunt, Chain, Two‑Handed)_

Anchor chain repurposed into a weapon of persuasion. The eight segments, forged from iron, each etched with a prayer that does nothing against a skull, are connected by links that rattle like the bones of the dead. The priests insist the prayers are essential. The shinobi who wield it are less concerned with the gods and more concerned with the sound of iron breaking bones.

- **Cost:** 350 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 20
- **Damage:** 16d12
- **Accuracy:** -3
- **Speed:** 18, Delay 6
- **Special:** Stun 4. Anyone attempting to parry the Hachiman Tetsu takes a -4 penalty to their parry roll. The chain segments allow the wielder to attempt a Disarm action against an opponent who successfully parries, using the same roll as the parry attempt.

---

### Tonfa

_(Blunt, Dual)_

The tonfa is a wooden bar the length of the wielder's forearm, with a handle one‑quarter of the way from one end. It is an unorthodox weapon, used to block, strike, and spin. It is also an effective weapon, used to disarm, deflect, and, when necessary, break. The tonfa's design allows it to be used in both offense and defense, transitioning seamlessly between the two. It is wielded in pairs, because one tonfa is a curiosity and two tonfa are a threat. The threat is usually not the tonfa itself, but the person holding it.

- **Cost:** 50 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 10
- **Damage:** 4d8
- **Accuracy:** +1
- **Speed:** 10
- **Special:** +1 to Parry, and you can parry unarmed attacks with a Tonfa. You may block weapon attacks and reduce their damage by 50% (rather than the normal 25%), though after doing so three times, the Tonfa breaks.

---

### Umbrella

_(Umbrella, Blunt)_

Yes, umbrellas. The ones used in combat are reinforced with metal or hardwood, making them semi‑decent bludgeoning weapons. Most have a sharp, pointed tip added at the end. They are also, on occasion, used to block rain. The weapon is common in the Land of Water, where rain is frequent and shinobi are practical. The umbrella is also common in the Land of Fire, where it is used to shield against the sun. It is not common in the Land of Wind, where there is nothing to shield against but sand. The sand does not care about umbrellas.

- **Cost:** 100 Ryo
- **Stamina:** 11
- **Damage:** 6d10
- **Speed:** 12
- **Special:** You may Block as a Speed 6 Interrupt with an Umbrella; when used this way, both you and the Umbrella take the (reduced, by blocking) damage from the attack. Umbrellas have 250 Durability; once it has taken that much damage, it breaks. Villages do not bother to repair broken weapons (and will not replace or repair them if bought with Savings), but can repair them if they have durability remaining.

---
title: "Rank Gear"
---

The difference between a genin and a jōnin isn't just authority but what the quartermaster will sign out to you without asking questions. Each rank unlocks equipment that would get a lower-ranked shinobi detained for an inventory audit. The descriptions below assume standard Confederacy issue; field modifications, black‑market upgrades, and captured enemy gear exist but are not catalogued here.

---

## Ninja

### Genin

#### Identification Tag

- **Cost:** Free
- **Tags:** Armor, Consumable

An oval brass disc, roughly the size of a watch face, stamped with your village emblem and service number. The edges are smooth from years of use; the kanji is often worn, but the meaning remains legible. A knotted cotton cord passes through a single hole at the top, worn diagonally across the chest, under the undershirt, so that it rests against the left side of the ribcage, close enough to the heart, as the old soldiers say. You feel it shift when you run, a constant, minor reminder that someone, somewhere, is keeping track. When worn on a body part (declared when rolling Initiative if not on the left chest), it absorbs the first Minor wound to that location, the plate cracks, the cord snaps, and the tag is knocked off in the process. The impact transfers through the brass; you still feel it, but you keep the limb.

---

#### Flak Jacket

- **Cost:** 200 Ryo per location (Arms, Legs, or Torso), or 500 for a full "suit"
- **Tags:** Armor, Consumable

A vest made of ballistic nylon and ceramic‑impregnated canvas, filled with segmented steel plates that slide and shift as you move. The jacket sits over the torso, covering the chest and upper abdomen; the full suit adds articulated sleeves and trousers that stop a few inches short of the ankles. The whole assembly weighs on your shoulders, not uncomfortably, just enough to remind you it's there. The plates are meant to catch shrapnel and small‑caliber rounds; they're less effective against focused blunt force, and useless against a sword thrust, but for fragments and debris, they're the difference between a scar and a closed casket.

Track coverage across three locations: Arms, Legs, Torso (includes Abdomen for wounding). Armor on a location covers both limbs. You may only have one type of armor per body location.

The first time you would be wounded in a given location, reduce the wound severity by one (potentially negating it entirely) and reduce total damage taken (after wound determination) by 20. The second time, reduce severity by one (to a minimum of Minor) and reduce damage by 10. After the second hit, the armor is compromised, no further protection, though the weight and bulk remain, until repaired at a village facility.

While worn, you take –1 to Athletics, Stealth, and Initiative per location covered. Removing a location's armor is a Speed 10 action in combat; removing a full suit is Speed 25. Out of combat, it's trivial.

Villages repair requisitioned damaged armor for free, but only once per OOC day. The armorer's desk has a logbook. The logbook has notes.

### Chūnin

#### Field Vest

- **Cost:** 400 Ryo
- **Tags:** Armor, Consumable

Canvas and ceramic‑plate body armor, issued to chūnin as a matter of course. The vest covers the torso and abdomen, with webbing straps over the shoulders and sides to attach ammunition pouches, a holster, or a radio. The ceramic plates are rated to stop small‑caliber rounds and disperse blunt force; the canvas absorbs the rest.

There is a jōnin version, slightly different shoulder cut, marginally better materials, but the functional difference is negligible. The real distinction is who signs the issue form.

When your torso or abdomen would be wounded, the vest reduces the wound severity by one and reduces final damage (after wound determination) by 50. The next time it protects you, reduce damage by 40; then 30; then 20; then 10; then 0. After the last reduction, the vest is too degraded to provide further benefit. You may only have one type of armor per body location.

---

#### Pattern‑14 Smoke Grenade (Custom)

- **Cost:** Level × 10 Ryo
- **Tags:** Consumable, Environmental

Chūnin have enough pull to request smoke bombs tailored to their operational preferences. The armorer will accommodate, provided the request is submitted in triplicate and the intended use does not involve testing the ventilation system of the armorer's office.

Base level starts at 0. Modify by adding the following:

- **Visibility Penalty:** –4 (+0), –6 (+2), or –8 (+3)
- **Duration:** 5 IC (+1), 10 IC (+2), 15 IC (+3), or 20 IC (+5)
- **Radius:** 5 yards (+1), 10 yards (+2), or 25 yards (+3)
- **Range:** STR/5 yards (+0), STR/4 yards (+1), or STR/3 yards (+2)

#### Demolition Charge, Type‑2

- **Cost:** 250 Ryo
- **Tags:** Consumable

The reason Type‑2 charges are not issued to genin has been the subject of multiple investigative hearings, all of which concluded with the phrase _training accident_ before the final page. They are functionally identical to standard charges in application, but the seal‑matrix is denser and the output is not. Damage is 7d20.open(10), area 30 yards. Deals double damage to inanimate structures—walls, bridges, fortifications, vehicle hulls. The laminated instruction sheet is no longer included.

### Jōnin

#### Parrying Gloves

- **Cost:** 100 Ryo
- **Tags:** Armor, Consumable

Leather gloves with reinforced knuckles and a segmented steel plate across the back of the hand and wrist. The plate is grooved to catch a blade's edge, not hold it, just deflect it. The leather is oiled, supple, and breaks into your hand shape over time. They smell faintly of gunpowder and leather conditioner.

These allow you to parry weapon attacks unarmed; however, if you do so, any wounds (if you fail) will be applied to your hands (or arms, as appropriate for the wound's severity). The first two times you're wounded doing this, the wound's severity is reduced one rank. The second time that happens, the plate on that glove is broken. Wearing both gloves (100 Ryo) allows a total of four such reductions. You may only have one type of armor per body location.

You may also block with these guards, with the same wound location and severity effects. This allows you to block weapons without reducing blocking's effectiveness.

---

#### Demolition Charge, Type‑3

- **Cost:** 500 Ryo
- **Tags:** Consumable

The end‑all and be‑all of 'mundane' explosives, reserved for jōnin simply because of how difficult they are to produce, which is in turn reflected in how troublesome they are to get ahold of. Type‑3 charges, like Type‑2, deal double damage to inanimate objects. They deal 10d20.open(10) damage, have an area of 50 yards, and deal triple damage to inanimate structures (walls, bridges, fortifications, vehicle hulls, but not jutsu‑based defenses). The instruction sheet has been replaced by a liability waiver.

### ANBU

#### Chimney Sweep Gas Mask

- **Cost:** 200 Ryo
- **Tags:** Armor

A white ceramic mask, smooth as a bird's egg, painted with stylized lines that suggest the face of an animal—a fox, a wolf, a crow, a badger. The eyeholes are black glass, the filter is a horizontal ribbed canister that covers the mouth and chin, and the whole thing weighs just enough to make you aware of your own breathing. The ceramic is brittle; the mask does not protect the head from blunt force — it is not armor. It is a statement. It says: I am not a person. I am not a shinobi. I am a function, and you will not remember my face.

ANBU masks reduce the severity of head wounds by one rank, a total of three times before being made useless. The mask cracks, crumbles, and falls off during this process. You can only have one type of armor on a given body location.

---

#### Covert Ops Cloak

- **Cost:** 200 Ryo
- **Tags:** Armor

A wool trench coat, cut long and dark, with a high collar that can be turned up to obscure the lower face. The material is treated to be water‑resistant and to suppress heat signatures; the black dye is deep enough to swallow light. It smells of lanolin and rain. The sleeves are cut wide enough to draw a concealed weapon; the pockets are deep enough to hold a signal rocket, a canteen, or a trauma kit. On the back, between the shoulder blades, there is a small pocket designed to hold a folded identification tag, in case the body is found without the coat.

Once per 100 IC you can make an automatic surprise attack with any Small‑size weapon, drawing it as a Speed 0 action, as long as you haven't used that weapon previously in the fight.

They come in black (for most ANBU) and white (for captains). Most ANBU captains stick with black; the white is too conspicuous, too much of a target. They're ANBU captains. They do what they want.

### Kage

#### Kage Hat

- **Cost:** Priceless

Every village's Kage has a distinctive hat to go with their formal Kage's robes. It is a peaked cap, high in front, sloping low enough to shade the eyes, with a chinstrap that is never fastened. A kanji indicating the country of origin is embroidered in gold thread above the visor; the hat's body is a color corresponding to the village (green for Konoha, blue for Kumo, gray for Iwa, etc.), with a white band at the base. If worn properly, and if you tilt your head slightly, the brim can obscure the upper half of the face. This is considered dramatic and is often done for photographs that will be printed on currency.

While not useful in combat, it is a very nice hat.

---

#### Kage Robes

- **Cost:** Priceless

Formal attire for Kages: a high‑collared, double‑breasted greatcoat in white and off‑white, with the lower half and lapels lined in a color corresponding to the village. The buttons are brass, the epaulettes are fringed, and the whole thing swishes and rustles dramatically when you walk. The coat is heavier than it looks, but it breathes, something about the weave of the wool and the cut of the armholes. It is designed to be worn over the standard field uniform, or over a dress shirt and tie if the occasion demands.

The robes impose a –3 penalty on accuracy, dodge rolls, and Athletics checks. However, because Kages are totally badass they can shed these robes as a Speed –2 Interrupt. And yes, that's a negative sign there. The coat hits the floor; the Kage is suddenly in their field gear; the photographer has to re‑focus.

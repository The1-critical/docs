---
title: "Vehicles"
---

Considered the modern successor to cavalry mounts, motor vehicles have become the standard for rapid deployment and heavy support in the Confederacy's military operations. Motorcycles, jeeps, armored cars, trucks, boats, and even light helicopters allow shinobi to cover vast distances, carry heavy equipment, and bring overwhelming firepower to bear. The drawbacks are equally obvious: engines are loud, vehicles cannot sneak, fuel is a constant concern, and a well‑placed Raiton jutsu can turn a truck into a smoking wreck. Still, for those willing to trade stealth for speed and armor, a vehicle is an invaluable asset.

Vehicles are purchased with Savings, though members of Major clans may use Requisition at half the listed price. You must have at least Rank 1 of the Leadfoot ability before purchasing or operating any vehicle in combat or hazardous conditions. Basic out‑of‑combat driving on open roads does not require Leadfoot, but the moment you enter initiative or face a Control Check, you need Leadfoot to avoid immediate loss of control.

## Vehicle Rules

### Boarding and Dismounting

Entering a vehicle (driver's seat, passenger seat, sidecar, cargo bed, or boat deck) is a Speed 5 action when you are within Range 2 of it. Dismounting is also Speed 5. You may dismount as a Speed +4 Link to any attack (including cqc, weapon, or vehicle‑weapon attacks). The vehicle continues moving under its own momentum for 1d4 IC after you dismount, then stops. If you dismount while the vehicle is moving at more than half its maximum Speed, you must make an Athletics TN 15 roll or land Prone.

---

### Vehicle Statistics

- **HP (Hull Points):** The vehicle's durability. When reduced to 0, it explodes (or sinks, for boats) – see Destruction.
- **DR (Damage Reduction):** Reduces incoming damage from all sources except Raiton, which ignores half the vehicle's DR (rounded down). For boats, DR applies only to attacks from above the waterline; underwater attacks (e.g., Suiton from below) ignore DR.
- **Speed:** Yards per IC on appropriate terrain (land or water). For amphibious vehicles, two speeds are listed (land/water). You use the vehicle's Speed instead of your AGI for movement.
- **Accuracy Penalty:** Occupants making personal attacks (thrown weapons, unarmed strikes, ninjutsu, etc.) suffer the vehicle's listed Accuracy penalty. Vehicle‑mounted weapons (see Vehicle Weapons) ignore this penalty.
- **Control TN:** Target number for Control Checks (see below). For boats, this applies to piloting in rough water or during evasive maneuvers.
- **Dodge:** The bonus of dodging while using your vehicle and base dodge stat, it increases the base speed of speed by +3 unless you have the Small unique.
- **Handling:** Bonus (or penalty) to Control Checks. Can be negative.
- **Seats:** How many occupants (including driver). Passengers may act normally unless the vehicle is _Enclosed_ (then they cannot be targeted directly).
- **Fuel:** A measure of remaining operating time. See Fuel Mechanics below.
- **Tags & Effects:** Special properties.

---

### Fuel Mechanics

Vehicles require fuel to operate. Each vehicle has a Fuel rating (typically 5–12). Fuel represents abstracted remaining operational capacity – not precise gallons, but a simple track of how long the vehicle can perform before needing resupply.

**Fuel Consumption:** Fuel decreases only during combat or strenuous use. Out of combat, normal travel does not consume Fuel (assume you refuel between missions). In combat, Fuel decreases by 1 at the end of each IC multiple of 20 (i.e., every time the Initiative Count reaches 20, 40, 60, etc.) while the vehicle is running. Additionally, the following actions each consume 1 Fuel immediately when performed:

- Making a Control Check (including those forced by damage or sharp turns).
- Firing a vehicle‑mounted weapon (each shot, not each reload).
- Taking damage that reduces the vehicle's HP by 25% or more of its maximum in a single hit.
- Being struck by a Katon technique of C rank and above causes fuel to reduce by -1 (the heat evaporates fuel lines).

When a vehicle's Fuel reaches 0, its Speed is halved (rounded down, minimum 1 yard per IC), and it cannot fire any vehicle‑mounted weapons that require fuel (all of them). It may still move at half speed. The vehicle cannot regain Fuel during combat.

Out of combat, you may refuel a vehicle by spending 50 Ryo per Fuel point (using Requisition or Savings). Refueling takes 10 minutes and can be done at any village or friendly outpost. You may also carry spare fuel containers (one container = 5 Fuel, costs 200 Ryo, weighs trivial, can be stored in cargo space). Refueling from a container is a Speed 20 action (out of combat, 1 minute).

---

### Control Checks

Whenever the vehicle takes damage (after DR) or you attempt a sharp turn (changing direction by more than 45 degrees) while moving at more than half its maximum Speed, you must make a Control Check: `1d20 + DEX/10 + Handling` vs the vehicle's Control TN. Failure means the vehicle skids out of control: it moves an extra 1d6 yards straight ahead (ignoring terrain hazards), then stops. All occupants are Stunned 2 and knocked Prone. For boats, a failed Control Check means the boat spins in place (no forward movement) and all occupants are Stunned 2 (but not Prone, unless thrown overboard – Athletics TN 15 to stay aboard).

No Control Check is required for normal straight‑line movement or gentle curves (less than 45 degrees over 10 IC).

---

### Combat While Mounted

- You use the vehicle's Speed for movement. You may move and attack in the same action.
- Melee attacks from a moving vehicle gain +0.2 PDB per full yard moved (as part of the attack), to a maximum of +50% of your base PDB. This bonus applies only if you are the driver or a passenger in an open vehicle; it does not apply from an enclosed cabin.
- Vehicle weapons use your base weapon accuracy and are treated as weapon taijutsu.
- Personal attacks (thrown weapons, unarmed, ninjutsu) made from the vehicle suffer the vehicle's listed Accuracy penalty. Vehicle‑mounted weapons ignore this penalty.
- Area of Effect attacks that include the vehicle also include all passengers (except those in an _Enclosed_ vehicle; they are protected until the vehicle is destroyed). The vehicle automatically blocks attacks directed at it (Speed 0 defense) using its DR.
- Occupants gain +2 Dodge while the vehicle is moving. This bonus is lost after your first attack in a battle. For open vehicles, this bonus is halved (+1) if you are in the passenger seat.
- Vehicles and their passengers are immune to Genjutsu, Bleed, Poison, Ignite, Burn, Shock, and Paralysis. Stun causes the vehicle to stop moving for its duration (the driver cannot act). Immobilization reduces vehicle Speed by 10% per point (max 90% reduction) and environmental visibility status from Ninjutsu are doubled in effectiveness.
- Raiton vulnerability: Lightning techniques ignore half the vehicle's DR and force an immediate Control Check (as if the vehicle took damage).

---

### Vehicle Destruction

When a vehicle's HP reaches 0, it is destroyed. For land vehicles, it explodes. For boats, it sinks and may explode if fuel ignites (50% chance). Resolve the explosion immediately:

| Vehicle Type | Explosion Damage (Energy) | Area |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motorcycle / Sidecar | 8d20 | 10 yards |
| Jeep / Light Truck | 12d20 | 15 yards |
| Urogane (Armored Truck) | 14d20 | 20 yards |
| Guntō (Light Tank) | 16d20 | 20 yards |
| Tengu (Helicopter) | 14d20 | 20 yards (plus crash) |
| Kamikaze (Biplane) | 14d20 | 20 yards (plus crash) |
| Speed Boat | 10d20 (if fuel ignites) | 15 yards |

Occupants may attempt to bail out (or abandon ship) as a Speed 4 Interrupt (Athletics TN 20). On success, they take half damage from the explosion and are thrown 1d6 yards (Prone, or into water if on a boat). On failure, they take full damage and are Incapacitated for 5 IC. For boats, if you fail to abandon ship before it sinks, you must swim (Athletics TN 15 each IC to stay afloat) or drown after 10 IC.

If a vehicle you own is destroyed, you cannot purchase another for 4 OOC days (one month IC), and your maximum Willpower is reduced by 1 during that time (grieving period). This applies even if you are Noble.

---

### Dismounting Attack

You may dismount as a Speed +2 Link to any attack (including those that cannot normally be performed while mounted, such as CQC Taijutsu). You may include your own attack‑move distance alongside the vehicle's movement for that action, but you do not gain the melee damage bonus per yard moved. The vehicle continues forward under inertia for 1d4 IC, then stops. From a boat, dismounting means diving into the water; you may then swim (half your normal movement speed) or be picked up later.

---

### Repairing Vehicles

Out of combat, you may repair a damaged vehicle. Repairing requires a Research roll (TN 20) and 10 minutes per 10% of maximum HP restored. You must have access to spare parts (costs 100 Ryo per 10% HP, or can be scavenged with a Survival roll TN 25, which takes 30 minutes and yields enough parts for 20% HP). A vehicle reduced to 0 HP cannot be repaired – it is destroyed. The Mechanic ability (see below) may improve repair efficiency.

---

**Types of Vehicle**

All vehicles have HP equal to the listed value (Vitality is not used; vehicles have HP only). Vehicles with no DR have DR 0.

### Kaze – Light Motorcycle

The Type‑17 Light Reconnaissance Motorcycle, field‑designated _Kaze_, entered production in the eighth year of the Confederacy. It was not designed for shinobi in mind but rather by a logistics officer who was tired of walking. The frame is welded steel tubing, painted matte olive, with a single‑cylinder engine that produces just enough torque to carry a rider and a light load. The suspension is crude but effective; the tires are knobby, civilian pattern, because military‑grade rubber is still allocated to vehicles that carry more than one person. Despite its humble origins, the Kaze has become the standard scout mount for every major faction. It is cheap, easy to maintain, and quiet enough that an attentive rider can hear an enemy patrol before they hear the engine.

- **Cost:** 800 Ryo
- **HP:** 400
- **Accuracy Penalty:** −2
- **DR:** 5
- **Speed:** 7.0
- **Control TN:** 18
- **Handling:** +2
- **Seats:** 1
- **Fuel:** 5
- **Effects:** Allows stealth level 1.

---

### Raiden – Military Sidecar Motorcycle

The Type‑22 Raiden was the Confederacy's answer to the need for a mobile fire support platform that didn't require a full jeep. The base motorcycle is a heavier version of the Kaze, with reinforced swingarm and a driven sidecar wheel to prevent dragging on soft terrain. The sidecar itself is a welded steel tub, painted in the same matte green, with a pintle mount for a Type‑99 Light Machine Gun. The gunner sits in the tub, exposed, with a belt of ammunition across their lap. The driver gets a slightly better seat and the dubious honour of being the first target anyone shoots at.

- **Cost:** 1200 Ryo
- **HP:** 600
- **Accuracy Penalty:** −3
- **DR:** 10
- **Speed:** 6.5
- **Control TN:** 20
- **Handling:** +1
- **Seats:** 2 (driver + sidecar)
- **Fuel:** 6
- **Effects:**
  - Passenger can fire a mounted Light Machine Gun (see Vehicle Weapons).
  - Driver takes −1 Dodge. Sidecar passenger cannot use melee attacks.

### Tsuchi – Light Utility Jeep

The Type‑30 Tsuchi is not glamorous. It is not fast. It is not well‑armored. What it is, is everywhere. Produced under license in nine different factories across the Confederacy, the Tsuchi is a four‑wheel drive utility vehicle with a canvas roof, fold‑down windshield, and bench seats for three passengers in the back. The engine is a liquid‑cooled inline‑four, reliable to the point of boredom. The chassis is box steel, welded and riveted, with leaf springs that have been known to survive landmine blasts. The Tsuchi is the standard patrol vehicle for chuunin squads, used for everything from logistics runs to combat insertion.

- **Cost:** 2500 Ryo
- **HP:** 1000
- **Accuracy Penalty:** −4
- **DR:** 15
- **Speed:** 5.5
- **Control TN:** 22
- **Handling:** +0
- **Requires:** Chuunin rank
- **Seats:** 4 (driver + 3 passengers)
- **Fuel:** 8
- **Effects:**
  - Ignores half of ground‑based immobilization penalties.
  - Passengers can make ranged Taijutsu attacks with no penalty, but suffer −2 Dodge (instead of +2) while moving.
  - Can carry up to 500 Ryo worth of equipment without penalty.

### Urogane – Armored Heavy Transport Truck

The Type‑44 Urogane is a mobile bunker with a steering wheel. Built on a six‑wheel heavy truck chassis, the Urogane features an armored cab with bulletproof glass slits and a rear cargo bay that can carry up to six passengers or a ton of supplies. The armor is riveted steel plate, tested against small arms and fragmentation; it will stop a light machine gun round at two hundred meters. It will not stop a direct hit from a tank shell or a point‑blank Raiton jutsu, but few things do. The Urogane is used for high‑value convoy escort, prisoner transport, and as a mobile command post in active combat zones. The engine is a diesel six‑cylinder, loud enough to announce your presence from half a mile away.

- **Cost:** 5000 Ryo
- **HP:** 2500
- **Accuracy Penalty:** −6
- **DR:** 40
- **Speed:** 4.0
- **Control TN:** 24
- **Handling:** −4
- **Requires:** Chuunin rank
- **Seats:** 2 (driver + front passenger) + cargo bay holds up to 6 additional passengers or 1000 Ryo of equipment
- **Fuel:** 12
- **Effects:**
  - Driver and front passenger are enclosed (cannot be targeted directly unless the cab is breached).
  - Passengers in the cargo bay are considered _Open Top_ (can attack, but suffer −2 Dodge).
  - When the Urogane rams an enemy (as a full action, Speed 10), it deals damage equal to its current HP / 4 (Blunt, Area 5) and the driver must make a Control Check (TN 24). On success, the target is knocked Prone and the truck continues; on failure, the truck stops.

### Guntō – Light Tank

The Type‑46 Guntō is the Confederacy's standard light tank, designed for infantry support and breakthrough operations. It carries a crew of four: driver, gunner, and two passengers who are technically loaders but in practice are additional infantry who ride on the hull. The main armament is a low‑velocity cannon, effective against buildings, light armor, and entrenched positions. The armor is sloped, riveted, and thick enough to shrug off most small‑arms fire and all but the heaviest ninjutsu. Raiton is the exception – lightning travels through the rivets and cooks the crew.

- **Cost:** 8000 Ryo
- **HP:** 3000
- **Accuracy Penalty:** −8
- **DR:** 60
- **Speed:** 3.5
- **Control TN:** 26
- **Handling:** −4
- **Requires:** Jounin rank
- **Seats:** 4 (driver, gunner, two additional passengers – passengers cannot fire weapons)
- **Fuel:** 10
- **Effects:** The gunner must use their action to fire the Main Cannon (see Vehicle Weapons). The driver controls movement. Ignores ground‑based immobilization entirely.

### Tengu – Light Helicopter

The Type‑51 Tengu ("Goblin") is a light observation helicopter, designed for reconnaissance, medevac, and light attack. The airframe is a welded steel tube with an aluminum skin; the rotor is a two‑blade semi‑rigid system, simple enough to be maintained by a field mechanic. The cockpit is a bubble canopy, offering excellent visibility and no protection whatsoever. In the Confederacy, the Tengu is the symbol of the new warfare: fast, fragile, and everywhere at once.

- **Cost:** 7000 Ryo
- **HP:** 2000
- **Accuracy Penalty:** −4
- **DR:** 25
- **Speed:** 6.0 (flying)
- **Control TN:** 24
- **Handling:** +0
- **Requires:** Chuunin rank
- **Seats:** 4 (pilot + 3 passengers)
- **Fuel:** 8
- **Effects:**
  - Ignores all ground effects (mud, caltrops, etc.).
  - Cannot be hit by melee attacks unless the attacker can fly or jump high enough (GM discretion).
  - Takes double damage from Raiton techniques (after DR).
  - Pilot or passenger may fire Rocket Pods (see Vehicle Weapons).
  - Can remain stationary at any altitude; while hovering, no Control Checks are required unless damaged.

### Hayabusa – Speed Boat

The Type‑40 Hayabusa is a light patrol boat used by the Confederacy's riverine forces and coastal defense units. The hull is welded aluminum, planing design, with a single inboard engine and a water jet drive for shallow operation. The boat is fast, nimble, and lightly armed—a single LMG mount on the bow, plus personal weapons for the crew. The Hayabusa is not designed for ship‑to‑ship combat; it is designed for interdiction, insertion, and extraction.

- **Cost:** 3000 Ryo
- **HP:** 800
- **Accuracy Penalty:** −4
- **DR:** 10 (only against attacks from above waterline)
- **Speed:** 7.0 (water only)
- **Control TN:** 20
- **Handling:** +1
- **Requires:** Chuunin rank
- **Seats:** 4 (driver + 3 passengers)
- **Fuel:** 6
- **Effects:**
  - Can only operate on water; cannot go on land.
  - Passengers gain +2 Stealth while on the boat (water reflections).
  - When moving at full speed, the boat leaves a wake that imposes a −2 Visibility Penalty for anyone behind it (up to 20 yards).
  - If the boat takes more than 50% of its HP in a single hit, it begins to sink. It will sink completely after 10 IC unless beached or repaired (requires a Repair action, Research TN 25, 10 minutes).

### Kamikaze – Light Reconnaissance Biplane

A sturdy, fabric‑covered biplane with an open cockpit, used for aerial reconnaissance, light ground attack, and dogfighting. Slower than the Tengu helicopter in raw speed but more agile in the air. Its open cockpit leaves the pilot exposed, but the plane's tight turning radius makes it a favorite for shinobi who value maneuverability over armor. The Kamikaze is used for visual reconnaissance, artillery spotting, and light ground attack in permissive airspace. It is not used for air superiority; that is a different conversation. The official manual states that the Kamikaze should not be flown in contested skies or used in dog fights. The official manual is not consulted by the people who fly them.

- **Cost:** 6000 Ryo
- **HP:** 1400
- **Accuracy Penalty:** −4
- **DR:** 20
- **Speed:** 8.0 (flying)
- **Control TN:** 22
- **Requires:** Jounin or Missing Nin rank
- **Handling:** +2
- **Seats:** 2 (pilot + rear gunner/observer)
- **Fuel:** 8
- **Effects:**
  - The pilot can fire twin fixed LMGs as a vehicle weapon (see Vehicle Weapons). These count as a single LMG for ammo and fuel consumption.
  - The passenger operates a swivel‑mounted LMG (same stats as LMG) with a 270° rear arc. The gunner may fire even while the plane is moving.
  - The plane may carry up to 2 light bombs (purchased separately; 100 Ryo each, consume 1 Fuel per bomb dropped). Dropping a bomb is a Speed 10 action; it lands at a point within 40 yards below the plane after a Delay 10. The bomb deals 10d20.open(6) Energy damage in an Area 10 (Targeted AoE). Bombs can only be dropped while the plane is at least 10 yards above the target.
  - If the plane takes a Control Check while moving at less than half its maximum Speed (4.0 yards/IC), the check suffers an additional −6 penalty (risk of stall).
  - The pilot and gunner are not enclosed; they can be targeted normally, and they gain only +1 Dodge (instead of +2) while the plane is moving.
  - Fragile Wings: Takes double damage from Raiton techniques (after DR) and from any explosion that hits the wings (GM discretion – usually AoE attacks from below or side).
  - Crash Landing: If the plane is reduced to 0 HP but not destroyed in a fireball (e.g., engine failure, fuel exhaustion), the pilot may attempt a forced landing: make a Control Check (TN 24). On success, the plane skids to a stop; all occupants take 4d20 Blunt damage and are Stunned 4. On failure, it crashes as per normal explosion rules (but with only half the explosion damage, no fuel ignition).
  - When making a Control Check to perform a sharp turn or evasive maneuver (like dodge) while an enemy aircraft is within 20 yards, the Kamikaze gains +4 Handling.

## Vehicle Weapons

Vehicle‑mounted weapons use the Firearm tag (see Equipment chapter) with the following clarifications: cannot be dodged, no DB × Speed, no damage bonus from stats, must reload after each shot (unless otherwise noted), called shots not allowed (except HMG). Reloading uses the listed Reload Speed and consumes one unit of ammunition (Cap and Ball, Small or Large). You may have as much ammunition as you wish (Requisition or Savings). Each shot also consumes 1 Fuel (in addition to any other Fuel consumption). Using vehicle weapons ignores the vehicle's Accuracy penalty.

| Weapon | Mounted On | Damage | Range | Reload Speed | Special |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Light Machine Gun (LMG) | Raiden (sidecar), Tsuchi (open top), Hayabusa (bow mount), Kamikaze (forward or rear) | 8d12.extra(4) | 20 | Stamina 8, Speed 12 | Passenger can fire as a normal action. May be fired while moving. Using a LMG does not consume Fuel. |
| Heavy Machine Gun (HMG) | Guntō (coaxial), Urogane (bed mount) | 12d20.extra(6) | 30 | Stamina 12, Speed 20 | Called shots allowed (normal called shot rules apply). Cannot be fired while the vehicle is moving at more than half speed. |
| Main Cannon | Guntō (turret) | 16d20.open(8) | 50 | Stamina 20, Speed 30, Delay 5 | Targeted AoE, Area 10. Cannot be used if the tank moved during the same IC (driver action). Requires gunner action. |
| Rocket Pods | Tengu (underwing), Hayabusa (rear mount) | 8d12.open(10) × 3 | 25 | Stamina 15, Speed 25, Delay 5 | Fires three rockets at same or different targets (multi‑throw rules). May be fired while moving. Each rocket consumes 1 Fuel (total 3 Fuel per salvo). |

**Note:** Vehicle weapons are considered part of the vehicle. You may move the vehicle and fire them in the same action _if you are the driver and the weapon is controlled by the driver_ (e.g., LMG on a sidecar requires a passenger; HMG on a roof requires a gunner). For weapons that require a separate gunner, the gunner uses their own action to fire. The vehicle movement and the gunner's action can occur on the same IC (simultaneous). Weapons with the _Cannot be fired while moving_ restriction (HMG) cannot be used in the same IC as movement, even by a passenger.

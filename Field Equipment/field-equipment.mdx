---
title: "Field Equipment"
---

The difference between Requisition and Savings, in practice, comes down to paperwork. Requisitioned equipment carries a village stamp, a serial number, and the implied understanding that it comes back when the mission does. Savings equipment is yours. If you die with it, someone will find it eventually and wonder where you got it.

## Consumables

### Demolition Charge

_[Genin Issue]_<br />**Cost:** 100 Ryo

Rectangular wax-paper slabs impressed with the seal-script that converts stored chakra into a directed detonation. The kanji in the center translates loosely as _ignite_, which is either the most functional or the most understated piece of labeling in military production depending on who you ask. They are issued to genin in packs of three with a laminated instruction sheet nobody reads after the first day. The priming process, focusing a small amount of chakra to cause adhesion—does not require formal training. The rest does. A primed charge can be affixed to most surfaces and set to detonate via a delayed-fuse action (Chakra 5, Speed 10, Delay X — X set between 10 and 10 + Chakra Control ranks). The delay cannot be aborted; once the count begins, the sizzling and smoke are visible to any shinobi who knows what to look for. Fire does not trigger detonation, and charges do not chain-react even if stacked.

- **Detonation area:** 20 yards
- **Damage:** 5d20.open(10), Blunt wounds
- Simultaneous detonations combine their damage for wound calculation. The explosion cannot be defended against, but anyone outside the area when the count ends avoids damage automatically.

### Demolition Charge, Type-2

_[Chuunin Issue]_<br />**Cost:** 250 Ryo

The reason Type‑2 charges are not issued to genin has been the subject of multiple investigative hearings, all of which concluded with the phrase _training accident_ before the final page. They are functionally identical to standard charges in application, but the seal‑matrix is denser and the output is not.

- **Damage:** 7d20.open(10)
- **Area:** 30 yards
- Deals double damage to inanimate structures — walls, bridges, fortifications, vehicle hulls.
- The laminated instruction sheet is no longer included.

### Combat Breacher

_[Jounin Issue]_<br />**Cost:** 500 Ryo

These are issued individually, signed for, and tracked by serial number. The ordinance office has noted, in internal documentation, that the rate at which Jounin charge packs go missing has increased steadily over the past six years and that no satisfactory explanation has been provided for the trend.

- **Damage:** 10d20.open(10)
- **Area:** 50 yards
- Deals triple damage to inanimate structures.
- The instruction sheet has been replaced by a liability waiver.

### Hemostatic Compound

**Cost:** 250 Ryo

Developed during the last decade of the war, when field surgeons were processing more casualties than they could triage. The compound is a tablet, dense and slightly chalky, with a bitter aftertaste that the manufacturer describes as _medicinal_ and anyone who's taken one describes as _regrettable_. It accelerates clotting and temporarily increases blood volume through mechanisms the Confederacy's medical research division has classified as proprietary.

- Taking one in combat is a Speed 10 action.
- For 50 IC afterward, you recover RES/10 HP per IC (not Vitality), take one‑third damage from poison, and take no damage from bleeding‑related effects.
- Limit: RES/50 per day.

### Crowfoot

**Cost:** 20 Ryo (Area 5 coverage)

Issued to infantry in small cloth bags. The design is pre‑war and hasn't changed: four spikes welded at a central node so that no matter how they land, one points up. Standard uses are predictable. Less standard uses involve anyone pursuing you through a market stall at speed.

- Deploy as a Speed 10 action, scattering to cover Area 5 up to STR/6 yards away.
- Anyone moving through at more than half speed takes 1d4 damage per IC spent in the area — no damage bonus, no wounding.
- Non‑consumable in the sense that the stakes can theoretically be retrieved; practically speaking they stay where you left them.

### Gauze Roll

**Cost:** 3 Ryo per yard

Two hundred yards per roll, heavyweight cotton, bleached. Technically civilian medical supply; in practice, the pattern is the same as Confederacy standard issue and no one in Natsugakure asks which distribution chain it came from. Bulky beyond the second roll. The standard practice among field operatives is to carry one roll and resupply from locals where possible.

### Military Canteen

**Cost:** 50 Ryo

Pressed aluminum, olive drab, belt clip. Holds 3 gallons. Two or more beyond the second apply a −1 dodge penalty each, not from weight, but from the way liquid shifts when you move. Can be used to reduce burns by −1 per canteen as a Speed 2 action.

---

### Field Jerry Can

**Cost:** 150 Ryo

The term originated in Iwa during the last mobilization and spread, which is either a comment on the can's quality or on the speed of military vocabulary exchange. Worn across the back. Holds 8 gallons. Limit of one; it takes the whole back. Can be used to reduce burns by −2 per canteen as a Speed 5 action.

### Stun Flash Grenade

**Cost:** 50 Ryo

Photostatic charge in a palm‑sized casing, pull‑ring fuse. Within Area 5 of impact, applies a −3 Visibility Penalty for 10 IC. Can be thrown as a Speed 4 action up to STR/5 yards.

Used as a Speed +4 link to a Dodge: the area centers on you rather than a target, applies to the attacker's accuracy, and lasts only for that roll. The original patent, filed by a Kirigakure engineer, describes the primary function as _suppression_. The secondary function, written in the notes but not the patent, is _extraction_.

### Wire Spool

**Cost:** 15 Ryo per 10 yards

High‑tension 16‑gauge wire on a flat military spool. The Confederacy manufactures this in twelve facilities across four territories and has never once run short. Available anywhere that anything is available.

### Field Cordage

**Cost:** 10 Ryo per 5 yards

Standard hemp rope. Individual lengths up to 100 yards; longer runs require joins. Ten yards restrains a person, assuming they're not resisting; tying while they are resisting requires either help or creative application of other techniques.

- **Tie quality:** Espionage roll, result or 20 as the escape TN.
- **Slip the knots:** Espionage + DEX/10 (5 minutes, can be concealed with opposed Espionage).
- **Snap the rope:** 1d20 + Athletics/5 + STR/10 vs 30 (Stamina 25; if you can do it, you can, once, no repeated attempts).

---

### Smoke Grenade

**Cost:** 50 Ryo

A tin canister the size of a fist, pull‑ring release, gray paint over whatever color it originally was. The Confederacy issues them in gray and off‑white; black market versions sometimes appear in green, which anyone who has run operations near Kiri identifies on sight and treats with appropriate caution.

- Throwing distance STR/5 yards, Speed 4.
- Creates a cloud in Area 5: −6 Visibility Penalty for 20 IC. The cloud does not follow the wind. The manual says this is intentional. The manual is optimistic.

### Combat Stimulant Tablet

**Cost:** 250 Ryo

The official Confederacy name is Standard Performance Supplement, Type‑4. Everyone calls them what they are. The Iwa chemical corps developed the formula during the final push; Konoha reverse‑engineered it within a year and now produces it in larger quantities with a slightly different tablet shape to maintain brand distinction. Every village has its own name, its own branding, and the same active compound.

- Speed 10 to take in combat.
- Immediately lowers Fatigue by one category.
- Provides +5 to Chakra Exhaustion and Stamina rolls for the next hour.
- Limit: one per battle, RES/50 per day. Taking more does not increase the benefit. Taking too many is how people end up in Natsugakure's underground clinic with a story they refuse to tell.

### Field Antivenom

**Cost:** Varies

Counteragents for common shinobi poisons. Available at roughly half the cost of the poison itself; additional doses follow the standard tiered pricing. Procurement through medical corps channels, village dispensaries, or Natsugakure's pharmacist who has never been seen twice in the same coat.

- Taking an antidote: Speed 6.
- Immediately cures the relevant poison and its effects.
- Side effect: −2 to Accuracy and all d20 rolls for 100 IC as the counteragent processes.
- Immunity to that poison for the duration.
- The narrow specificity of antidotes is a known limitation; the medical corps has been studying a broad‑spectrum solution for eleven years and the progress reports have become shorter each quarter.

### Field Compass

**Cost:** 50 Ryo

Lensatic military compass, hinged cover, brass case with a glass face. Points approximately north on stable ground; performs poorly near large concentrations of Raiton chakra or underground infrastructure.

- The map and compass together add +4 to Survival rolls.
- The compass alone adds +2.
- Neither helps if you don't know where you started.

### Trauma Kit

**Cost:** 25 Ryo

Standard‑issue canvas pouch, red cross stenciled on the flap (the cross has become a universal marking; nobody is confused about what it means and nobody shoots at it in front of witnesses). Contents: wound dressings, antiseptic tabs, clotting compound, splint materials.

- Stops bleeding from wounds: Speed 10 per wound category.
- Cures Burns or Bleed status: Speed equal to severity × 2. Single cure from a kit before it's consumed for that function.
- Treat a wound (reducing ongoing penalties): Speed 15 per category, consumes the kit.

### Signal Rocket

**Cost:** 40 Ryo

A pressed‑metal cylinder with integral propellant, launch handle, and striker cap. Fires upward or at an angle. In daylight it's a streak of light visible for several hundred yards to anyone watching. At dusk or into night, it can be seen for miles. Available in white, red, and green; faction significance varies by region and changes often enough that reading a signal chart more than six months old is considered operationally optimistic.

- The flash is painful to look at directly. Anyone within Area 5 of the launch point who doesn't shield their eyes takes a −3 Visibility Penalty for 5 IC.
- The standard use is signaling. The non‑standard use depends on who's asking.

---

### Blackout Torch

**Cost:** 40 Ryo

A small electric torch with a lens hood that reduces its visible light profile to a narrow beam. Used extensively during the war for navigation in blackout conditions; civilian surplus now fills most of the market.

- Battery life: thirty minutes.
- Spare batteries: 20 Ryo, another thirty minutes.
- The torch itself has no consumable tag; the batteries do.

### Chemlight

**Cost:** 20 Ryo

A sealed plastic cylinder containing two reactive compounds separated by a glass divider. Strike it against your knee to break the divider, shake to mix, observe the result: an hour of quiet, non‑directional, non‑electrical illumination in whatever color you selected. Cannot be turned off once activated. Cannot be dimmed. Draining the fluid by breaking it produces a glowing puddle rather than less light. Available in green, blue, amber, and red; red is occasionally used by medical teams as an indirect indicator of triage zones.

### Tactical Survey Map

**Cost:** 50 Ryo

Printed on heavy oilskin paper with topographic markings, settlement data, and road classification. Comes in a pressed‑tin case. Useful for navigation, tracking, and identifying likely routes of approach.

- +4 to Survival rolls in the mapped country, within the limits of what a map can tell you: where someone went, not necessarily who they are or what they want.

### Field Respirator

**Cost:** 200 Ryo

The M8 pattern has been in production for twenty years and looks like it. Rubber facepiece, dual canisters, adjustment straps that always need one more click.

- Putting one on in combat: Speed 5.
- The fit is uncomfortable enough to impose −3 to all Stamina rolls while worn.
- Benefits: immunity to external suffocation, survival underwater, +8 to Resistance rolls against inhalation vectors (the seal is not perfect).
- Connects to a compressed air cylinder via standard‑thread fitting.

---

### Compressed Air Cylinder

**Cost:** 100 Ryo · Consumable

Thirty minutes of breathable air per cylinder, pressurized, aluminum construction. Connects to the M8 respirator. Attaching one takes one hand and about five seconds outside of combat; in combat, treat it as a Speed 5 action.

### Field Reconnaissance Camera

**Cost:** 750 Ryo

Precision optical instrument in an anodized aluminum body. Variants include the standard handheld model, a head‑mounted monocular issued to Sunagakure scouts, and a chest‑mounted version favored by Kirigakure's intelligence teams for reasons that have to do with deniability of operation rather than image quality. The flash works in low light and produces a flare visible to anyone within thirty feet who's paying attention.

- Film cassettes are mission‑specific; development facilities exist in all major villages and one location in Natsugakure where the operator does not ask about the subject matter.

---

### Intelligence Film Cassette

**Cost:** 50 Ryo · Consumable

Light‑sensitive medium on a standard‑thread spool. Non‑interchangeable between manufacturers despite stated standards. Exposed cassettes must be developed; a village facility turns them around overnight for urgent intelligence requests and within one week for anything labeled _personal_. The categories are not always as distinct as either party pretends.

### Assault Grapnel

**Cost:** 100 Ryo

Three‑prong steel hook on a throw‑ring, designed to catch architecture, pipe, or natural features when lobbed over a wall or across a gap. Standard kit for academy infiltration training; rarely used by experienced shinobi who have better options but occasionally invaluable when those options aren't available. The rope must be purchased separately.

---

### Infiltration Entry Kit

**Cost:** 100 Ryo per +1 bonus

A set of small precision tools in a canvas roll: tension wrenches, picks, rakes, a diamond tip for pin‑tumbler mechanisms. Using them requires the Espionage skill to get meaningful results; the kit contributes up to +5 to that roll, limited by the user's skill ranks. The picks can break on a result of 5 or lower, sometimes successfully, sometimes not. Lockpicking in a hurry, under pressure, in low light, is why experienced operatives prefer not to be in a situation that requires it.

---

### Squad Radio

**Cost:** 500 Ryo (standard) · 1000 Ryo (extended range)

The current standard field communications device: a compact transceiver worn as a headset with a throat‑contact microphone. Press‑to‑transmit, always‑receive. Range of half a mile on the standard model; two miles on the extended. Tunable across a dozen preset frequencies with the turn of a dial, though anyone sufficiently motivated and positioned correctly can locate your frequency given sustained use and active scanning.

- A Major or worse head wound while wearing the radio destroys it.
- The device is sensitive enough to pick up breathing and movement if the user forgets it's on. Everyone in Natsugakure has learned this the difficult way at least once.

---

### Observation Binoculars

**Cost:** 200 Ryo

Coated optical glass, military‑specification focusing knob, rubber‑armored body. Effective to five hundred yards for general observation; legible text at one hundred yards with good light. Awareness rolls at these ranges take no distance penalty. A fixture of every observation post, checkpoint, and window overlooking the third gate at Natsugakure.

## Infiltration

### Field Concealment Kit

**Cost:** 150 Ryo · Consumable

Terrain‑specific. Available in four patterns: woodland, desert, mountain/rubble, and _other_ (the fourth category has expanded considerably as the theaters of operation have diversified). Application takes fifteen minutes.

- Result: concealment at Stealth level 3, with the roll made after you're in position.
- One use per kit.
- The village stamps are different; the compound is the same.

---

### Entrenching Tool

**Cost:** 40 Ryo · Non‑consumable

A folding military shovel. The blade hinges and locks via a quarter‑turn collar; the handle is wrapped in canvas that starts olive and ends whatever color the ground has been. Issued at induction. Carried until the handle splits. Most veterans have an opinion about the correct way to sharpen one.

- Can be wielded as a weapon, Speed 8, Stamina 8, (1 + DEX/10)d6 Blunt, uses Weapon Accuracy. It is not a good weapon. It is a shovel being used as a weapon, and the distinction matters.
- Digging a fighting position takes 30 minutes of sustained work. A properly dug position accommodates one person and provides cover that reduces incoming ranged attack damage by 20% and grants +2 to defensive rolls against ranged attacks. A second person can share the position but only one benefits from cover at a time. Positions can be prepared before a mission and used throughout it.
- Clears caltrops from an area at twice the normal speed.
- Can also be used to dig under or through barriers that are soil rather than stone. The Natsugakure Mining Quarter has tunnels dug with these. So does the old Confederacy fortification on the eastern road, though the shovels used there are a different stamp and a different decade.

### Incendiary Grenade

**Cost:** 80 Ryo · Consumable<br />**Tags:** Environmental

A cylindrical tin grenade, slightly heavier than a smoke, with a red pull‑ring rather than the standard gray. The fill is a Katon‑aspected accelerant compound that the Confederacy's chemical division describes as _thermally reactive chakra‑emulsified incendiary gel_ and that everyone in the field calls _the red one that burns_. Detonation is not a bang—it's a rush of sustained fire across the contact area.

They are available from Confederacy armories, intermittently, and from sources in Natsugakure that do not discuss acquisition. The ones with _SUNAGAKURE CHEMICAL WORKS — BATCH 7_ stenciled on the casing have a better reputation than the unmarked ones; field operatives who have used both do not strongly dispute this.

- **Throw:** Speed 4, up to STR/5 yards.
- **On impact:** Creates a burning Area 5 around impact. Anyone in the area takes 4d6 Energy damage and must make a Resistance TN 14 roll or gain Ignite 4.
- The area remains burning for 20 IC. Anyone in the area at the start of their IC or moving through it takes 2d6 Energy damage and must resist Ignite as above.
- Cannot be dodged (area effect). Moving out of range before detonation avoids damage entirely.
- Environmental flammables—dry timber, paper, bridges, stored fuel—ignite at GM discretion and may spread.
- A water technique of D‑rank or higher extinguishes the area. Rain reduces the burning duration by half.
- Raiton does not trigger incendiaries, which is a meaningful distinction from demolition charges and one that careful operatives exploit when planning.

### Propaganda Leaflet Bundle

**Cost:** 10 Ryo per Bundle (roughly 100 sheets) · Consumable

Low‑grade paper. Ink that bleeds in heavy rain. The content varies—a statistical comparison of Confederacy pension structures and actual disbursements, a list of the names of missing operatives never officially acknowledged, a grainy reproduction photograph of something that may or may not show what the caption claims—but the format is consistent: dense, authoritative‑looking, formatted as though it came from somewhere official.

Sometimes it did. Sometimes the header is _VOICE OF THE PEOPLE_ in a font designed to suggest a printing press older than the one that produced it.

They come from every faction. They are designed to look like they came from the opposing one.

- **Scatter:** Speed 4 action, Area 15, up to STR/5 yards. Creates a Propaganda Zone for 30 IC.
- **Civilian effect:** Non‑combatant civilians who enter the zone are occupied reading, arguing about what they've read, or picking up sheets to show someone else. They do not proactively assist either party in the combat and do not notice routine shinobi activity (not combat, not direct confrontation) within the zone.
- **Morale effect:** Trained soldiers and conscripts (not shinobi) who fail a Resistance TN 12 roll suffer −2 to Stamina and Chakra Exhaustion rolls for the remainder of the battle. Trained shinobi are not affected—they know what a leaflet is and what it's for.
- **Cover dressing:** Carrying a selection of leaflets from multiple opposing factions is the fastest way to present as politically unaffiliated in any setting where allegiance is under scrutiny. +2 to Espionage rolls to pass as a civilian neutral in such contexts.
- **Concealed seals:** A character with the Fuuinjutsu ability may embed a single D‑rank seal within a printed bundle during preparation. Anyone who picks up and reads the seeded leaflet becomes subject to the seal's effect. Detecting the seal requires Espionage or Chakra Control against TN 15 + the seal‑user's Chakra Control ranks.

title: "Western Province"
---

The Western Province has been unstable since the assassination of the Kazekage, with ongoing investigations shrouded in corruption and secrecy. The Kazekage, known for his reformative ideas like military reductions and economic redistributions, was viewed as a threat by established powers. His assassination coincided with a fire that destroyed the summit hall, raising suspicions of involvement by Konohagakure, particularly its covert organization known as Root. Despite Konoha’s denials, intelligence agencies prepared for possible implications.

In the aftermath, Sunagakure fell into chaos as the Regency took control, leading to the rise of the Crowns, an insurgent group that challenges the Regency's authority by arguing it embodies the corruption of the old order. Both factions claim the legacy of the Kazekage but misinterpret his true intentions. The war has taken on a non-traditional character, with neutral cities clandestinely funding both sides, mercenaries frequently changing allegiances, and assassinations becoming normalized.

Simultaneously, Iwagakure has been expanding, framing its growth as stabilization rather than conquest. Their philosophy argues that decentralized control leads to instability, and thus they believe their protection agreements are beneficial for smaller nations. This expansion has transformed several states into Iwa's economic domains, with local leaders publicly asserting their autonomy while privately negotiating economic structures with Iwa.

However, this expansion is proving costly, leading to labor strikes and rising internal taxes as Iwa's economy struggles under the weight of maintaining its growing territories. The Tsuchikage's administration, nonetheless, sees the destabilization of Konoha as a significant geopolitical opportunity, poised to assert greater influence in a region rife with turmoil and shifting power dynamics. The situation underscores the complex interplay of politics, ideology, and military strategy currently defining the Western Province and its surrounding territories, highlighting key location-specific entities like Konohagakure and Iwagakure, and the impact of the Kazekage's assassination on regional stability.

## **[Major] Akado**

The Akado Clan's fore-fathers, for many generations, had applied their sage-like role in the shinobi world toward pursuing a greater understanding of chakra and its many uses. Their research led to a terrifying mastery over chakra's very nature, dissembling, channeling, scattering, and gathering it with surgical precision. Originally an isolated sect within the Land of Sound's orbit, the Akado retreated further into isolation into the village hidden in the rain after the fall of their sponsors, creating hidden monastic temples focused purely on the perfection of chakra control. They have recently emerged, driven out not by a natural disaster, but by the slow, suffocating resource drainage orchestrated by the Southern Province's economic policies. Now residing precariously within the Leaf-dominated realm, the Akado offer their unique, neutralizing skills as powerful, highly-paid contractors whose loyalty is dictated purely by the highest bidder and the guarantee of fresh, untainted resources, making them the ultimate mercenary political leverage.

## **[Major] Fūma**

The Fūma clan's relationship with the Uchiha is the kind of family history both sides have learned to stop bringing up at formal occasions. Distant enough to be deniable, close enough to matter, the Fūma shared a bloodline with the greatest doujutsu users in the world and manifested nothing. No Sharingan. No tomoe. No legendary eye that sees everything.

What they got instead was an obsession. If the Uchiha read a battlefield through their eyes, the Fūma learned to read it through their skin. Centuries of shinobi who could not see what was coming learned instead to feel it, the pressure change before a thrown weapon arrives, the subtle shift in air current that betrays a hidden opponent before a technique is ever formed. They called this sense Kaze no Me. They didn't name it after an eye by accident.

The Fūma clan distinguishes itself as engineers, carrying versatile components during campaigns. Unlike the Uchiha, who were granted authority over Konoha's Military Police Force after a massacre, the Fūma took their role seriously while being alive and welcomed in Sunagakure. Their well-equipped compound reflects their commitment to policing. Recently, the Fūma engaged in the Sunagakure civil war as neutral scouts and engineers, providing services to the highest bidder. Their designs, including ballistae and wind-tower relays, have made them pivotal yet resented by both factions. Fūma archers maintain neutral, often refusing to attack fellow clansmen, upholding their policy: build for anyone, kill for no one, earning them a reputation as quiet profiteers in the ongoing conflict.

- **Other Nin**: Never trust a Fūma who offers to calibrate your siege weapons. They'll do it perfectly, but six months later they'll sell the exact same blueprint to your enemy.

All Fūma are trained in Fuuton ninjutsu during their cadet training. They start with the Elemental Aptitude ability for Wind for free.

It operates through the body's surface: the skin, the sinuses, the fine proprioceptive awareness of a shinobi who has trained their nervous system to process atmospheric data. When active, an observer close enough to look will notice a faint silvery luminescence at the outer corners of the user's eyes, a pressure artifact from the chakra circulation required to sustain it, not a doujutsu in any conventional sense. The Fūma are aware this makes the comparison to their cousins uncomfortable for everyone involved.

Activating Kaze no Me is a Speed 0 action, performed alone or alongside another technique or Interrupt. It has an Upkeep of 1. You may activate it when rolling Initiative with the Preparation ability.

Kaze no Me requires unimpeded chakra circulation across the body's surface. Suffering Immobilization 6 or higher disables all Kaze no Me abilities for its duration.

Like their rivals the Uchiha, the Fuma are not inherently able to access this power. Rather, in times of need (generally when under severe combat-related stress) the Kekkei Genkei will activate itself. This is GM-regulated; you’ll be informed if this takes place. When it does, your Kaze no Me immediately activates at the stage above the highest you’ve achieved, and from then on you may activate that stage at will.

## **[Major] Henzaki**

Due to their perfect biological compatibility, Henzaki members formed the medical team that handled the Kazekage's assassination and autopsy. They are the only ones with reliable, first-hand knowledge of the forensic evidence and the nature of the poison or weapon used, making them the ultimate, living witnesses in the Konoha vs. Internal Treachery debate. This singular event has violently dragged their subtle existence into the center of the civil war. Possessed of a rare, yet subtle, kekkei genkai, Sunagakure’s Henzaki clan is virtually unknown outside of their village. Even within Sand, the most an average person knows about them is that they have some manner of secret technique or bloodline which makes them hard to kill. This is their bloodline: an incredible, self-regulating mechanism that allows them to survive against adversity. Injuries which should be fatal prove only a minor inconvenience to the Henzaki. Henzaki virtually never get ill, and never seem to age past their forties. Critically, the Henzaki’s bloodline makes them all universal donors and universal recipients for blood transfusions and organ transplants. Currently, they are designated "Forensic Assets" by the Regency. The corrupt leadership exploits their unbreakable resilience, assigning them as lone, uninterruptible couriers of sensitive intelligence and contraband between Sunagakure and its Iwa-funded mercenaries. Their knowledge makes them the most important living secret in the Western Province.

## **[Major] Hitori**

The Hitori were a clan living on borrowed time, possessing the elemental power of Scorch Release (Shakuton), a devastating fusion of Fire and Wind that allows them to instantly dehydrate and incinerate matter. This destructive nature led to their ancestral homes being pushed to the outskirts of Sunagakure, far from fertile lands. Their power earned them the grim designation as the village's "Sanitary Corps": they were the ones tasked with cremating plague victims, robbers condemned to die, battlefield casualties, and failed experiments, a duty few others in Suna could stomach, making them isolated but essential.

Hitori themselves were once the essence of energy and youth, but the perpetual exposure to death and their own arid chakra has left them emotionally hardened. They are now the Regency's primary rapid deployment force for internal suppression. They are currently consumed by bitterness and are fueled by a recent, devastating event: During the initial chaos of the Regency's crackdown, a Crown raiding party mistakenly believed the Hitori's isolated ancestral home was a Regency storage depot for illicit goods. The Crowns launched a devastating night raid, resulting in the massacre of the Hitori elders and non-combatants. The Hitori who returned found their ancestral home reduced to ash and parched earth, a scene they themselves were unable to purify.<br />

Now, their bitterness is absolute. Unwelcome guests within the Wind Country are run off by the Hitori, who use their abilities in a subtle fashion to create living conditions that quickly become unbearable. They are now the perfect weapon for the Regency: vengeful, emotionally distant, and utterly efficient, fighting their private war under the guise of loyalty. However some want infighting to end, killing kin and destroying lives isn't what their jovial forebearers wanted. <br />

--( **Rumor Has It** )--

- **Sand Nin**: The Hitori are a necessary cruelty. They leave no evidence, either of the enemy or of their own broken kin. Their grief has been weaponized; they now hate the rebels more than they value their own lives. We give them names, locations, and autonomy, and they reduce the problem to sterile ash.
- **Other Nin**: Do not engage a Hitori operative. They are fighting for vengeance, not politics, and they will not stop until your body is dust. Hitori are the only clan in Suna that literally cremates their own dead because nothing else can cleanse their bitterness

Hitori begin with Elemental Aptitude for Fire and Wind ninjutsu, along with the first rank of Elemental Affinity: Shakuton, which includes Katon and Fuuton.

**-=[ Shakuton - Scorch Release ]=-**

Combining Fire and Wind chakra results in the advanced nature of ‘Scorch Release’; as the name suggests, it involves using extreme heat as a weapon, with powerful users being able to reduce opponents to ash or evaporate the liquid inside their body, sapping away their strength.

Any Katon or Fuuton technique can be converted to Shakuton as a Chakra +5, Speed +0 link, with the following changes:

- Deal 75% normal damage, but can not be blocked
- They gain +(Rank/2) to their Accuracy, rounded up
- Inflict a Dehydration status equal to their Rank (E = 1, D = 2, etc)
- Shakuton can increase a victim’s Dehydration status up to a maximum of 3+(CHA/8)
- Shakuton can not inflict Burns or Ignite
- Fuuton converted to Shakuton may not be used with a Fan.
- Any Ignite a technique would normally inflict instead adds +5% to Wounding per severity of Ignite
- Any jutsu converted with an inherent bonus to Wounding instead applies a Stun of (Wounding Bonus%)/20, rounded down
- Shakuton techniques can not be reduced below Speed 5, after all other modifications

Shakuton is weak to Suiton, and strong against Fuuton and Doton

**–( Dehydration )–**<br />This is a Status Effect, applied on hit. Every point of Dehydration applies a -1 penalty to the victim’s Stamina rolls, and lowers the victim’s Physical Damage Bonus by 0.3. Dehydrate from the same source (person) stacks fully with itself; if you suffer Dehydrate from different people, only the highest severity applies.

There is one exception: Dehydrate’s Stamina penalty does not stack with Burns, and if you happen to be suffering from both status effects, you take the higher of the two, rather than both, to Stamina rolls: If you were suffering from Dehydration 2, and received a Burns 3, your Stamina penalty would be -3, and your PDB penalty would be -0.6. If you then cleared your Burns status, the Stamina penalty would lower to -2.

Any effects which reduce Stamina penalties reduce the severity of your Dehydration by the same amount. However, unlike Burns, Dehydrate is not cleared when failing a fatigue roll, and instead it is only reduced by half (rounding up) of its original value.

Outside of battle, this status effect goes away at a rate of one per minute.

## **[Major] Sabaku**

The Sabaku are an elite lineage of the Land of Wind who reject formal clan organization, traditionally introducing themselves by their "Sabaku no" followed by their given name, which roughly translates to "(name) of the Sand Waterfall," signifying loyalty to the Sand above any family house. They split from the Monkasei, the main house, but retain their Kazekage lineage.

Their defining bloodline technique is Sand Release, a specialized expression of Magnetic Release that allows them to manipulate sand and mineral-rich materials such as Iron Sand and Gold Dust. Refined over generations, Ryūsaton has made the Sabaku vital to the Sand's defense industry and economy. The recently assassinated Kazekage, Meji Monkasei, was considered the lineage's "Prophecy Child." His wit and wisdom led many to believe he would guide the Confederacy into an era of peace. His assassination, widely blamed on Konoha, shattered that hope and divided the lineage.

One faction, dominant among the Crowns, demands revenge against Konoha. The elders instead suspect the conspiracy may lie within Sunagakure itself and fear an attempt to eradicate the Sabaku bloodline.

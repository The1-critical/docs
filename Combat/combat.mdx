## Initiative

Combat begins with all participants making an Initiative roll: 1d20 + (AGI)/10. From there, everyone is assigned a number depending upon how well they did compared to one another.

Everyone subtracts their roll from the person who rolled the highest; this is the initiative count they will act on.

_Example: Choji, Shikamaru, and Ino are preparing to fight. Choji rolls 6, Shikamaru 16, Ino 12. They act on the following initiative counts:_

- _Choji: 16 – 6 = 10_
- _Shikamaru: 16 – 16 = 0_
- _Ino: 16 – 12 = 4_

People who join a combat after it has begun simply join on the IC the combat has advanced to upon their arrival. If multiple people join at once, they all roll initiative as normal; the highest joins at the current IC, and others join a number of IC later as determined above.

---

## Flow of Time

Combat begins on Initiative Count 0 ("Init 0" or "IC 0"). Players act whenever their initiative count is reached.

Every action has a given Speed. When you take an action, its effects resolve immediately, and its Speed is added to your current initiative count – that is when you will be able to act again.

_Example: If you use a Speed 6 action on IC 5, your action resolves immediately. After it finishes, combat progresses to IC 6, others take their turns, and so on, until IC (5+6 =) 11 is reached, when you act again._

Some effects may lower an action's Speed. No matter how many such effects are applied, no action's Speed may ever go below half its original value (rounded up). Additionally, no action's final Speed can be reduced below 3. An action with a base Speed of 3 or lower cannot have its Speed decreased at all.

### Simultaneous Actions

If two people act on the same initiative count, both actions are declared and resolved simultaneously. You and your opponent will both fully declare and resolve your attacks (you can take turns for convenience), but do not apply their effects until after both have been declared, at the very end of the IC. The exception is Fatigue rolls: you roll and resolve them immediately in the midst of that IC, in any order. Penalties or changes from advancing a Fatigue level do not apply to any other rolls for that IC (such as rolling a Dodge after advancing Fatigue from your own attack).

Order of resolution on simultaneous ICs: Attacks resolve first, followed by any triggered effects that depend on those attacks (such as Cunning Blow's bonus on the next attack after a successful defense). This means if two characters act on the same IC and both attack each other, both attacks are resolved before either character's defensive success can trigger an offensive bonus for the same IC.

Variable Speed Actions (such as Block, Observe, or Guard) are partial exceptions: if you were utilizing such an action when your turn came up, and you choose to use it again as your simultaneous action, it takes priority – you may use Block (or Total Defense, Guard, etc.) as a defense against an attack delivered on the same IC under those circumstances.

### Action Points

One of the things that makes battles dramatic is sudden, unpredicted surprises – and among shinobi that’s par for the course. Mechanically, this is represented by Action Points; thematically, this can be a burst of adrenaline or the result of carefully luring your opponent into position for a counter‑attack.

You gain 5 AP every time the Initiative Count reaches a multiple of 20. You start battle with zero AP, and your maximum AP is DEX/2.

You can spend AP whenever you take an action. Each AP spent reduces the action’s Speed by 1.

## How to Fight

### Attacking

The simplest and most effective way to resolve a conflict is often violence. The first step is to declare your attack: its name, Speed, cost(s) (Stamina or Chakra), and Accuracy, so the defender knows what they face and can decide how to respond.

_Example: Choji rushes toward Shikamaru and swings a heavy fist toward his face! (Basic Unarmed, Speed 8, Stamina 5, Accuracy based on STR/10)_

### Accuracy

Accuracy is a target number (TN) your opponent must match or beat on their defense roll. Your attack’s Accuracy depends on the type of attack:

- **Close Quarters Combat** (unarmed Taijutsu, grapple, touch-range attacks (yes even ninjutsu)): 10 + (STR)/10 plus any bonuses from abilities, uniques, or the technique.
- **Weapon and Ranged Taijutsu**: 10 + (DEX)/10 plus bonuses.
- **Ninjutsu**: 10 + (CHA)/10 plus bonuses.

Accuracy Rolls: Sometimes you must make an Accuracy Roll – replace the base 10 with a 1d20 roll. The formula is 1d20 + (appropriate stat)/10 + other modifiers to that Accuracy type. When something penalizes both that Accuracy type and d20 rolls, apply only the penalties to Accuracy; if it penalizes only d20 rolls, those penalties apply.

<br />

### Damage

Every attack has a Base Damage – a number of dice of a certain size. For example, a Basic Unarmed Attack does 2d4 base damage. The actual damage formula is:

**(Base Damage) + (Damage Bonus) × (Speed) + (any other bonuses)**

Your Damage Bonus is determined by STR for CQC, CHA for Ninjutsu, and DEX for Weapons divided by 15 and rounded to one decimal place. This bonus is multiplied by the attack’s final Speed – slower, more solid attacks benefit more from raw power. Unless noted otherwise, any alteration to an action’s Speed also affects the Speed used for the damage bonus multiplication. 

All bonuses to Damage Bonus are additive, not multiplicative. For example, using a two‑handed weapon (+33%), Chakra Flow: Fire (+25%), and Weapon Focus (+1 flat) on a Burning Spear that already uses NDB instead of WDB gives: (CHA/15) × (1 + 0.33 + 0.25) + 1, not (CHA/15 × 1.33 × 1.25) + 1. Flat bonuses (like Weapon Focus ranks) are added after multiplication.

Damage is applied to an opponent’s Vitality until it reaches 0. Any overflow, and all future damage, goes directly to their Hit Points.

<br />

### Handseals

Most Ninjutsu and Genjutsu have a Seal Speed entry. A Seal Speed of 0 means that while handseals are required, you can perform them so fast that the time required is negligible For others, you must first take a Perform Handseals action (see Basic Actions). The jutsu must be used on your next action; its effects resolve then, and your next action is determined by the jutsu’s Speed.

### Genjutsu

Genjutsu are mind‑affecting, often illusory techniques. Attempting to influence someone with Genjutsu is resolved through opposed Genjutsu rolls.

- **Attacker:** 1d20 + XP/400 plus any offensive Genjutsu bonuses.
- **Defender:** 1d20 + XP/200 plus any defensive Genjutsu bonuses.

If the defender rolls lower, they suffer the technique's effects.

Bonuses to "defensive rolls" (e.g., Sixth Sense, Uchiha's Observe) do not apply to defensive Genjutsu rolls. Bonuses to Accuracy do not apply to offensive Genjutsu rolls. The following apply to Genjutsu rolls: effects that explicitly mention Genjutsu rolls, effects that apply to all d20 rolls, and Fatigue penalties.

Each time a Genjutsu is successfully used against you in combat, you gain a cumulative +5 bonus to defensive rolls against that specific jutsu for the rest of the battle.

**Breaking Genjutsu:**

- Genjutsu Kai (D-rank Ninjutsu).
- Suffering a Wound of any severity ends all Genjutsu effects on you.
- Many Genjutsu have upkeeps or durations; details are in the jutsu entries.

If you fail a Genjutsu roll by 5 points or fewer, you realize you've been affected by an illusion. If you fail by more, you have no reason to suspect anything is wrong.

Genjutsu used in combat end automatically when combat ends, except for AoE Genjutsu with upkeeps (excluding clones), those that put someone to sleep or remove them from combat, those that require helpless targets, and those that inflict Stun.

### Interrupt

Certain actions carry the Interrupt tag, which allows them to be used in response to another action, typically an attack directed at you — rather than on your own turn.

You may use an Interrupt if its Speed (or, for ninjutsu with Seal Speed, its Speed + modified Seal Speed) is half or less the Speed of the action you are interrupting. This is the one situation where you may use a ninjutsu that requires handseals without having performed them beforehand, the seals and the technique resolve together as one interrupt.

The interrupt's full Speed is added to your current IC to determine when you next act, the same as any other action.

**Defensive interrupt Speed floor:** Unlike normal actions, defensive interrupts are not bound by the standard minimum Speed of 3. A Dodge (Speed 2) can be reduced to as low as Speed 1. The absolute floor for any defensive interrupt is Speed 1.

Speed increases from Wounds (such as Bruised Ribs) or status effects (such as Paralysis) that apply to your defensive interrupt can be fully cancelled out by AP spending, and do not raise the minimum Speed, a Dodge with +3 from Paralysis can still be reduced to Speed 1 with sufficient AP. This allowance does not apply to Links added to a defensive interrupt: a Dodge + Kawarimi chain has its minimum Speed set by the chain as a whole, not the Dodge alone.

In the rare case that a defensive interrupt itself becomes an attack, such as when a parry technique converts on success, the standard minimum Speed of 3 applies to that resulting attack.

### Partial Success

When you fail a defensive roll, for each point you fail you take 20% of the attack's normal damage. Status effects from the technique apply normally.

_Example: You roll a 14 on your Dodge against an Accuracy 15 attack. Fail by 1 → 20% damage. Roll 12 → fail by 3 → 60% damage. Roll 7 → fail by 8 → 100% damage._

For defensive jutsu that work like Dodge or Parry (make a d20 roll; success avoids the attack), partial success applies normally. If the technique instead reduces damage by a set amount on success, partial success does not apply.

### Damage Reduction

Damage Reduction (DR) reduces incoming damage directly. If you have 10 DR and an attack deals 100 damage, you take 90. DR does not apply to status effects or to effects that do not deal damage.

Effects that reduce or bypass DR combine additively. If one effect bypasses half and another bypasses half, you completely bypass DR. If combining a flat reduction (e.g., 5 points) with a percentage reduction (e.g., half), apply the flat reduction first, then halve the remaining DR.

### Mitigation Order

As it’s inevitable that you will have multiple ways to reduce damage when hit, you apply effects in the following order:

1. Attacker’s modifications to damage (additive).
2. Partial Success (if applicable).
3. Damage Reduction.
4. Blocking.
5. Any other percent‑based reductions (additive).

“Additive” means percentage bonuses are added together into a single multiplier. Example: +25% and +33% gives DB × (1 + 0.25 + 0.33) = DB × 1.58. A CQC Specialist using Sudden Attack, CQC 2 (‑20%) gives (base dice + DB × Speed) × (1 + 0.1 – 0.2) = ×0.9. <br />

### Action Qualities

Chains are a special type of action, where multiple techniques are combined or used at once. Each chain has a single Core action, and one or more Links. Your core action can be virtually any action in the PHB, from dodging an attack to using a high-ranked ninjutsu. What makes an action a Chain is, instead, its Links. Links are techniques which, rather than being used alone (or sometimes as an alternative to that), can be used to supplement or modify another action.

The easiest way to identify a Link is that rather than its own Speed value (like "Speed: 4"), it will say something like "Speed: +4". Many of these also have Stamina or Chakra costs written as a number to be added or subtracted. In the description of these techniques, they'll specify what they do, and when they can be used (for example, Kawarimi is linked to a Dodge action).

Using a chain is fairly simple. Simply sum up the Speeds and costs of all actions involved; these are the values for the Chain, which is treated as one single, complete action. For example, when you apply Kawarimi to a dodge, "Dodge + Kawarimi" is your new action, with a base Speed of 6, and a Seal Speed of 4. Any effects which modify an action's Speed (or other variables) do so to the Chain itself, not individual links (or the core action).

In the case of Chains where the Core action is a damage dealing attack, this becomes slightly more complicated. The Speed value used for your (Speed)\*(appropriate Damage Bonus) will be the lower of the Core action's base Speed, or the Chain's final Speed (after all modifications), whichever is lower. The rank of the Core technique determines the rank of the chain; chains using Shuurai (a D-rank Raiton) would always be treated as D-rank attacks, even if you added an A-rank link to them.

There is no limit to how many links a chain may have. You could, for example, make a Chain out of Leaping Shadow Evasion (Core), Phantom Step (Link), Water Splash (Link) and, after that failed, Kawarimi (Link), all for a single dodge, so long as its Speed was still valid.

This action would have a Chakra cost of 15 (10+5, from Kawarimi and Mizuhane), and a fairly high Stamina cost (depending on how much effort you put into your Phantom Step).

**How to form an Action Chain for dummies!**

1. Choose (Virtually) any Action from the PHB.
2. Select a Link. Speed+(x), Stamina or Chakra +(x)
3. Tally the total Speed and Cost of your Chain.
4. Unleash the Beast!

### Delay

An action with Delay (e.g., Speed 12, Delay 4) is declared on your turn, but its effects occur 4 IC later. You may abort up to the last IC of the delay. Speed cannot be reduced below Delay, and Delay cannot be lowered unless an effect specifically states otherwise.

### Abort

You may abort an action on any IC before your next action, allowing you to act on the following IC. Aborting as part of an interrupt adds 1 to that interrupt's Speed.

### Variable Speed Actions

Actions like Block, Guard, and Total Defense have no set Speed, you declare a duration in IC. They have the Abort property by default. Any Stun amount forcibly aborts any Variable Speed Action you are performing. Effects that modify Speed (AP, Celerity, Wounds, Paralysis) do not apply to Variable Speed Actions.

### Willpower

You may spend Willpower after making an attack or a d20 roll, after determining success/failure, but before seeing results. You cannot spend Willpower after damage is rolled. Any given action may have Willpower applied only once, even if it involves multiple rolls.

**Effect:** Reroll the relevant roll (or, for an attack, make an Accuracy Roll). If the d20 result is less than 11, add 10 – this is your new result. Willpower cannot reroll Stamina or Chakra Exhaustion rolls.

### d20 Rolls

Bonuses to “d20 rolls” apply to:

- Defensive Rolls
- Skill Rolls
- Genjutsu Rolls
- Grapple Rolls

They do not apply to CQC Accuracy, Ninjutsu Accuracy, Weapon Accuracy (rolled or TN), Fatigue rolls, or damage rolls.<br />

### Basic Actions

These are actions anyone can perform in combat.

#### Basic Unarmed (Speed 8, Stamina 5)

A catch‑all for punches, kicks, and other body‑based attacks. Deals 2d4 base damage. This uses your CQC Accuracy, and your CQC Damage Bonus.

#### Basic (Weapon) (Speed Varies, Stamina Varies)

An ordinary attack with a weapon. The base damage, Speed, and Stamina cost are listed in the weapon’s Equipment entry. This uses your Weapon Accuracy, and your Weapon Damage Bonus.

#### Block (Variable Speed Action)

Declare that you are blocking and state a duration in IC. Until your next action, you may use the Block defense against any incoming attack, even those that normally do not allow a defense (e.g., Environmental tag, Explosive Tags). Block negates the Surprise Attack quality on attacks you block. Blocking reduces CQC and ninjutsu damage by 50%, but Weapon damage by only 25%. While blocking you are immune to the sixth sense degrade from surprise or sneak attacks. 

#### Dodge (Speed 2, Interrupt)

Roll 1d20 + AGI/10 + other dodge bonuses against the attack's Accuracy. Success means you completely avoid the attack and its effects.

#### Move (Variable Speed Action, Abort)

You travel (AGI × number of IC spent moving) / 50 yards. When attacking, you may move toward your target a distance corresponding to half your attack's Speed, for free. Movement cannot be reduced by AP.

#### Multi-Throw (Speed Varies, Stamina Varies)

Throw multiple weapons of the same type; shuriken or kunai, in a single action.

- Minimum 2 weapons, maximum DEX/10.
- Speed = 3 + (number of weapons)/2.
- Stamina = 5, +1 per 2 weapons total (6 for 2–3, 7 for 4–5, and so on).
- Minimum total Speed is 5 — this applies to both the base Speed and the final Speed after AP spending.
- Base damage = total of all weapon dice. Six shuriken = 6d6.
- Weapon Damage bonus increases by +0.5 per thrown weapon.
- Multi‑throws reduce the severity of all wounds they cause by one category, to a minimum of Minor.
- If multi-throwing shuriken, their inherent +2 Weapon Accuracy bonus applies only once regardless of how many are thrown.
- You must throw only one weapon type per action, shuriken or kunai, not a mix.

#### CQC Parry (Speed 3, Interrupt)

Deflect an attack with bare hands. You need at least one hand free to perform a CQC parry, and CQC parries can only be used on CQC attacks. CQC Parry uses a CQC Accuracy Roll.

When Fatigue penalizes both Accuracy and defensive rolls, CQC Parry suffers only the defensive roll penalty. If something penalizes only Accuracy, CQC Parry suffers that penalty.

#### Weapon Parry (Speed 3, Interrupt)

Deflect an attack with a weapon. You need to be actually holding a weapon in order to perform a Weapon Parry. Weapon Parry uses a Weapon Accuracy Roll.

When Fatigue penalizes both Accuracy and defensive rolls, Weapon Parry suffers only the defensive roll penalty. If something penalizes only Accuracy, Weapon Parry suffers that penalty. 

#### Perform Handseals (Speed Variable, Abort)

For jutsu with a Seal Speed greater than 0, you must perform handseals. The modified Seal Speed is Seal Speed – DEX/10 (minimum 0), which becomes the Speed of this Variable Speed Action. You may choose to forego part or all of your DEX/10 reduction. Handseals require both hands. Interruption (damage, being grappled, or any forced movement) aborts the action; you must start over.

If you take damage while forming handseals, make a Chakra Control skill roll against TN 10 + (damage dealt / 10). Failure aborts the action. Unlike other Variable Speed Actions, Stuns do not end your Perform Handseals action.

A successful Called Shot targeting the arms or hands while you are performing handseals increases the Chakra Control check TN by an additional 10.

While performing handseals, your hands are not free for other actions (parrying, grabbing, drawing weapons, or a separate Perform Handseals action for a defensive interrupt) unless you have the Single‑Handed Seals unique.

#### Rest (Speed 5)

Reduce your Stamina penalty by 1 (minimum 0). Rest's base Speed cannot be reduced, and you cannot use AP to reduce its Speed.

#### Take the Hit (Speed 6, Interrupt)

Protect an ally. Make a Dodge roll with +5 bonus. Success: you leap in the way. Failure: your ally takes 20% damage per point you failed by (you take the remainder). If you were Blocking as a Variable Speed Action when you use this, you may block if you succeeded by 1 or more. You may use other interrupts to defend yourself (except dodge or any dodge related interrupts), up to a maximum Speed equal to the margin of success. Cannot ordinarily be used against AoE attacks, see Defensive Formation ability.

### Stealth

#### Hiding

You may hide under one of four circumstances:

- Nobody you are hiding from is watching you.
- Everyone you are hiding from has a -6 or higher visibility penalty.
- You are already hidden and take another Hide action for a higher Stealth TN.
- A jutsu explicitly allows you to hide (then Hide action becomes Speed 0).

##### Hide (Speed 10)

Make a Stealth skill roll; the result is your Stealth TN. You are hidden at Stealth level 1. Each time you hide in the same battle, future Hide rolls take a cumulative -5 penalty. If your Stealth TN reaches 0 or below, you are no longer hidden.

##### Improve Hiding (Speed 7)

Performable only while at Stealth level 1. Move to Stealth level 2. Cannot take you from level 2 to level 3.

##### Stealth Levels

- **Level 1:** No TN bonus. You may Dodge, Block, Parry, Move, Search, Scan, Observe Clones, Aim, Meditate, use Items, perform Handseals, make Surprise Attacks, and use Genjutsu.
- **Level 2:** +5 TN bonus. May Search, Scan, Observe Clones, Aim, Meditate, use Items, perform Handseals, make Surprise Attacks, use Genjutsu, and prepare/perform Sneak Attacks. Cannot move.
- **Level 3:** +15 TN bonus. May Search, Scan, Observe Clones, Meditate, and prepare Sneak Attacks. Cannot move.

If you take an action not allowed at your current level, you drop to the highest level that permits it. Breaking stealth removes you entirely.

#### Searching

##### Search (Speed 6)

Roll Awareness against the Stealth TNs of hidden enemies. Equal or greater → you find them. Each consecutive Search without a non‑Interrupt action in between gives a stacking +2 bonus. You do not automatically know if someone has found you.

##### Point Out (Speed 3)

Indicate an enemy's location. Anyone else present may make a Search action immediately as a Speed 0 Interrupt with an additional +5 bonus.

#### Actions from Stealth

- **Surprise Attack:** Any attack from stealth. Ends your stealth (unless it is a non‑jutsu ranged weapon attack, which reduces your Stealth TN by 5 instead). Opponents defending may not spend AP to reduce their interrupt's Speed.
- **Prepare Sneak Attack (Speed Variable, Abort):** Line up a perfect attack. To use a subsequent action as a Sneak Attack, the total Speed you spent preparing must be at least equal to that action's Speed. If you abort or take another action first, you lose the preparation.
- **Sneak Attack:** Improved Surprise Attack. All inherent Accuracy penalties of the technique or weapon are removed (other penalties remain). You may interrupt any action with a Sneak Attack; the target may either complete their action and be automatically hit, or call off their action (pay no cost, next action determined normally) and defend normally. Sneak Attacks interrupt as if their Speed were 1/4 of its actual value. For jutsu with Seal Speed above 0, you must first Prepare Sneak Attack for Seal Speed + Speed IC, then abort preparation to Perform Handseals. You must use the jutsu on the IC you finish handseals, or it is lost.
- **Genjutsu from Stealth:** Can be used from levels 1 and 2 without breaking stealth, but never as a Surprise or Sneak Attack. Genjutsu requires the target to perceive you cannot be used while hidden.

### Cut Yourself (Speed 3, Stamina 0)

You draw a slashing weapon across your own flesh, a shallow cut on the palm, forearm, or another accessible area, to draw fresh blood. The pain is sharp but manageable, and the blood flows freely. In the heat of battle, this act serves a purpose beyond self-harm: blood is a catalyst, a component, and a symbol. For those who understand its value, a few drops can mean the difference between victory and defeat.

**Effects:**

You inflict 10 points of Slashing Hp damage upon yourself. This damage cannot be reduced, blocked, or prevented by any means. The wound is superficial and does not count as a Wound for the purposes of Wound thresholds, it is merely a shallow cut meant to draw blood, not to debilitate.

Upon successfully cutting yourself, you gain the following benefits:

- **Blood for Summoning:** You satisfy the blood offering requirement for the Summoning Technique (Kuchiyose no Jutsu) and your summons reduce their seal speed by -3
- **Blood for Sealing:** If you possess the Seal Training talent, you may use your own blood as a reagent for any Fuuinjutsu technique that the jutsu cost and seal speed are reduced by -5.

This action may not be used if you are at 0 HP or if the weapon you are using cannot inflict Slashing damage. The wound does not count as a Wound for the purposes of breaking Genjutsu (it is too shallow). You may only perform this action once every 50 IC, as repeatedly cutting yourself without rest is both foolish and ineffective.

## Conditions and Status Effects

### [Wounded](/Combat/wounds)

See the Wounds section. Effects are listed per wound, and healing times are under Wound Recovery.

### Fatigued

See the Fatigue section.

### Incapacitated

HP at 0. Helpless – cannot take actions or defend effectively. An enemy can knock you unconscious or deliver a coup de grace.

### Unconscious

As Incapacitated, plus cannot perceive the environment.

### Dead

Two ways: reduced to -100% HP, or a coup de grace (Speed 10, Delay 5 melee action against Incapacitated/Unconscious target; Speed cannot be reduced).

### Status Effects

#### Airborne

Airborne represents being lifted into the air, violently or otherwise. An Airborne ninja ignores all Ground-tagged effects and ground-based environmental hazards, but similarly cannot benefit from effects that require contact with the ground, and cannot be knocked Prone. They may still defend themselves, but their move speed is reduced to 0, potentially impacting their ability to escape areas of effect. The status ends on the Airborne target's next non-interrupt action, unless stated otherwise.

#### Bleed

Lose Vitality or HP equal to double severity every 5 IC. Stamina penalty equal to half severity (rounded down). Reduce severity by RES/20 every 20 IC (minimum 1). Bleeds from the same source do not stack; different sources stack damage but only the highest severity applies the Stamina penalty.

#### Burns

Generally resulting from exposure to fire or intense heat, such as some katon jutsu, being burnt makes it harder to fight effectively. Your Stamina penalty is equal to severity. If you get multiple burns: only the strongest applies. Effects that reduce Stamina penalties reduce all burn severities by the same amount; advancing a Fatigue category clears all burns.

### Buried

If you dig a hole to give you the buried status and are not seen this gives you a chance to hide similar to a visibly penalty would.

Buried is a status caused by the usage of doton jutsu. You can be buried up to a rating of ten. At buried one through five you can attack and be targeted. Higher than this you can not attack or be targetted. Unless you used a jutsu that says otherwise (or have oxygen tank!), you are at suffocation 1 when buried more than five.

Buried Status applies a immobilization penalty equal to twice its rating. You can dig yourself out of being buried equal to the immobilization penalty you are suffering in speed. Which means buried 4 would require a speed 8 action to dig yourself out.

#### Grounded

Grounded represents being submerged at least partially in the ground. A Grounded character's move speed is reduced to 0, and any penalties inflicted on them by the Prone condition are doubled, until they remove themselves from the ground as a Speed 10, Delay 5 action. Buried characters cannot be knocked Airborne, and applying the Buried condition removes the Airborne condition.

#### Ignite

You are on fire. Literally, you are burning. This is probably one of the most excruciatingly painful experiences of your life. 

Ignite's effects are somewhat complicated. Like most statuses, it has a severity. You suffer a -severity/2 penalty to your Stealth checks, and every 5 IC you take damage equal to its severity, plus its severity in percent of your current Vitality. Thus, an Ignite 10 would give you -5 to your Stealth and would deal 10 damage, plus 10% of your current Vitality. Each time it does damage, you subtract (RES/20) from its severity.

If you have no Vitality remaining, it instead does damage equal to its severity in percent of your maximum HP. The above Ignite 10 effect would deal 10% of your maximum HP in damage.

If someone ignites you again while you are already on fire (seriously, what kind of people are you fighting?), the more severe effect replaces the less severe one.

#### Immobility

Penalty to defensive rolls equal to severity, half (rounded down) to CQC, Ninjutsu, and Weapon Accuracy, and reduces AGI for movement by 10% per point. Maximum effective penalty -10. Multiple immobilizations do not stack. Area‑based immobilizations end when you leave the area; grapple‑based locks end when the grapple ends. Otherwise, fade at 1 per 10 IC. 

#### Paralysis

Paralysis is an internal effect which impedes your ability to properly use your own muscles, temporarily causing spasms and twitches that prevent you from going full force, which many ninja like to do, as it turns out.

When paralyzed, you treat the damage from your attacks as if they were (Paralysis severity) lower, to a minimum of half their final, modified Speed.

For example, at Paralysis 3 your Speed 8 Basic Attack would deal damage as if it were a Speed 5 attack; if you spent AP to reduce its Speed to 4, it would then be treated as a Speed 2 attack for its damage (because it can’t reduce it by more than half, and its Speed is 4).

Paralysis effects do not combine. If you are afflicted with two paralysis effects at once, the more potent one overwrites the less severe one. If they both have the same severity, the one with the longer duration overwrites the other.

#### Prone

-3 CQC, Ninjutsu, and Weapon Accuracy, -2 defensive rolls, half movement speed. Stand up as Speed 5 action. Being knocked prone while airborne causes immediate fall to ground

#### Poison

Make Resistance roll against the poison's Toxicology roll. Failure: take Severity damage per IC for the duration. Multiple poisons can exist simultaneously, but only the highest Poison and one set of Special Effects apply. Same source only renews duration.

#### Shock

Similar to Paralysis but more of a general impediment to one’s ability to move and act, Shock effects slow down ordinarily fast-moving, fast-acting shinobi, making it easier to outmaneuver an enemy before they can recover.

Shock increases the effective Speed of all non-Variable Speed Actions by its Severity; a Shock 3 would increase a Basic Unarmed Attack from Speed 8 to Speed 11, but would deal damage based off of its original Speed (in this case, 8). If it then had its Speed reduced (such as with AP), it could then be reduced to a minimum of Speed (11/2 = 5.5) 6 with AP, and deal damage as a Speed 6 attack; in essence, AP spent counteracts this status effect until you begin to lower its Speed below that of its unshocked amount.

Shock does apply to Interrupt actions, but at only half its severity, rounded down. Performing a Dodge (normally Speed 2) with a Shock 4 would increase its Speed to 4 (meaning that some extremely quick, previously-avoidable attacks might become undodgeable).

Shock effects do not combine, and no action can have its speed more than doubled regardless of your Shock severity. If you are afflicted with two Shock effects at once, the more potent one overwrites the less severe one. If they both have the same severity, the one with the longer duration overwrites the other.

#### Sleep

Helpless, but automatically wake and defend against danger at -5 penalty until first action after waking.

#### Stun

Delays your next action by the magnitude. Multiple stuns do not stack – higher overwrites. Stun ends when you take your next action. If removed early, the IC delay is removed but cannot bring your next action earlier than the current IC. Stun forcibly aborts any Variable Speed Action you are performing.

#### Suffocation

Penalty to Stamina and Chakra Exhaustion rolls. Every time the severity increases, make a Stamina 15 roll (with penalty equal to new severity). Not reset by Fatigue advance. Fades at 1 per 5 IC when suffocation stops.

#### Visibility

Penalty to Accuracy and Awareness equal to severity, half (rounded down) to defensive rolls. Maximum effective penalty -10. Does not stack – higher overwrites. Closing eyes is Speed 0 (not an Interrupt).

### Other Effects

#### Area of Effect (AoE)

Attacks that affect everyone within an area. To dodge, determine how far you could move in the attack’s Speed. If that distance exceeds the radius, dodge normally. If not, you suffer a -2 Dodge penalty per yard you are short. CQC and Weapon AoE attacks can be parried, but a successful parry only protects the parrier, others in the area are still affected.

#### Knockback

Measured in yards. When an effect knocks you back, partial success reduces the knockback distance by the same percentage it reduces damage, though this does not apply to AoE knockbacks centered on an individual, such as Kaiten or Eye of the Storm. Being knocked back through a ground-based environmental effect does not apply that effect to you mid-travel, only where you land matters. Being knocked back into or through a solid barrier (such as a wall created by Earth Shore Return) does deal its damage and effects normally, as you are physically contacting the barrier.

#### [Weather](/weather)

The world of the shinobi is not only shaped by the hands of man and the flow of Chakra as the untamed forces of nature itself has a thing to say. The elemental nations are defined by extremes, from the bone-chilling blizzards of the North to the scorching sandstorms of the West and a true warrior must master not only their own techniques but the very environment they fight in. Check the weather section and learn more horrors this world has to offer. 

### Clones and Conditions

Any clones you summon are subject to the same statuses and conditions you are suffering at the time of summoning (including wounds).

## Fatigue

### Stamina

When you use a move with a Stamina cost, make a Stamina roll: 1d20 + RES/10. Regardless of the result, your action resolves normally. If the roll is less than the Stamina cost, your Fatigue increases by one category.

Each Stamina roll adds a penalty to future Stamina rolls equal to (Stamina cost)/5, rounded down, with a minimum of 1. These penalties stack. The total resets to 0 when you advance a Fatigue category or at the end of battle. You cannot reduce a Stamina cost below 5 if its unmodified cost was 5 or higher.

### Chakra Exhaustion

When you use a move with a Chakra cost, make a Chakra Exhaustion roll: 1d20 + CHA/10. Regardless of the result, your action resolves normally. If the roll is less than the Chakra cost, your Fatigue increases by one category.

Each Chakra Exhaustion roll adds a penalty to future Chakra Exhaustion rolls equal to (Chakra cost)/5, rounded down, with a minimum of 1. These penalties stack. The total resets to 0 when you advance a Fatigue category or at the end of battle. You cannot reduce a Chakra cost below 5 if its unmodified cost was 5 or higher.

### Upkeep

Some techniques have an Upkeep cost. While maintaining them, you suffer a penalty to all Stamina and Chakra Exhaustion rolls equal to the total Upkeep of all active techniques. You cannot have multiple Upkeeps from the same source (e.g., two Chakra Strengthenings).

If your total Upkeep exceeds your total bonus to Chakra Exhaustion rolls from all non‑Fatigue sources, you automatically advance one Fatigue category.

_Example: Your CE bonus from non‑Fatigue sources (abilities, attributes, etc.) is +18. If you have Upkeep 19, you advance one Fatigue level, which then gives an additional +5 bonus (now +23) – but the advancement has already happened._

You may end any Upkeep at will, even when it is not your action.

### Fatigue Levels

You begin battle at Fatigue 0. Whenever you fail a Stamina or Chakra Exhaustion roll, your Fatigue increases by one category, and your roll penalties reset to 0. For every 15 points you fail by, you advance an additional Fatigue category.

If an action requires both Stamina and CE rolls, roll Stamina first. For multiple rolls, roll from lowest cost to highest.

_Example: Failing a Stamina 45 roll with a result of 12 means you failed by 33. The first failure advances one category; 33/15 = 2.2, so two additional categories, three total._

| Fatigue | Accuracy/Offensive/Skill | Defense | Stamina/CE Bonus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 1 | -1 | -2 | +5 |
| 2 | -2 | -4 | +10 |
| 3 | -3 | -6 | +15 |
| 4 | -4 | -8 | +20 |
| 5 | -5 | -10 | +25 |

Fatigue only goes to 5. If you fail a roll at Fatigue 5, you complete your action and then fall unconscious. If you fail by enough to advance beyond Fatigue 5, the technique fails to activate (in addition to you falling unconscious).

## Wounds

When a single source of damage deals more than a certain percent of your maximum Vitality (or HP), you suffer a wound of the corresponding severity.

| Severity | % of Max Vitality | % of Max HP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minor | 10% | 25% |
| Major | 25% | 50% |
| Severe | 50% | 75% |
| Critical | 75% | 100% |

If damage carries over from Vitality to HP, combine both portions to determine wounds. Example: 600 Vitality, 200 HP, take 100 damage. 60 Vitality (10%) → Minor wound, 40 HP (25%) → second Minor wound. Two Minor wounds.

**Wounding bonuses:** Effects that increase wounding do not change actual damage taken — they increase only the effective damage used to determine wound severity. This comparison is made after all reductions (DR, blocking, percent reductions) have been applied. An attack that deals 100 damage after reductions with +25% Wounding is compared against thresholds as if it dealt 125 damage, but the target only takes 100.

### Damage Types

After determining the severity of a wound, the next (fairly simple!) step is determining what type of wound it is. Being cut by a sword and being hit by a magical ninja fireball, even if they do the same amount of damage, have significantly different effects on the human body. For the most part this is self-explanatory, with only four damage types recognized: Blunt, Slashing, Piercing, and Energy.

CQC attacks are always blunt damage, nice and simple. Weapons have their damage type, or types, listed in their descriptions. If a weapon can do multiple types of damage, the attacker declares which type of wound they’ll be inflicting after the severity is confirmed, but before the location is determined. Some weapon jutsu require a weapon of a specific damage type to be used; in this case, damage from that jutsu is considered to be of that type.

Ninjutsu are simpler than weapons! Katon and Raiton jutsu inflict energy damage. Doton and Suiton inflict blunt damage. Fuuton inflicts slashing damage. An exception! Some jutsu augment a weapon strike, or actually create a weapon. In these cases, use the damage type of that weapon, not the element it was made from. 

### Location

After determining severity and damage type, you roll to determine location–where you hit, and what you injured. It's all well and good to say you threw a punch at their face, but this is a battle with ninjas! Things rarely go as planned. You roll a percentile die (1d100) for location, and check the relevant damage type's wound list, under the appropriate severity, to determine the effect. If it's an arm or leg that's been wounded, odds on the 1d100 are the right arm, and evens the left. This all gets simpler if the defender is blocking; in that case, they choose the location, which takes precedence over any Called Shot from the attack.

Multiple wounds, even multiples of the same effect, stack. Two broken left arms means that your arm is broken twice, with double the normal penalties.

One exception. If you suffer a critical wound, it overrides all lesser wounds on that location–having an arm reduced to useless deadweight makes the fact that it's broken in six places kind of, well, irrelevant. Two criticals in the same place, however, is oh so very possible.

In the cases of attacks which inflict multiple wounds at once, you roll for location only once and apply all wounds to the location(s) according to the wound type and severity on the Wounds page. If someone uses Block against such attacks, they can choose whether to apply them to the same location, or to separate ones.

### Called Shots

A called shot is an attack specifically targeting a certain location. After checking your attack’s damage type, choose any of the locations which can be Wounded by damage of that type. You declare that when you make your attack, for example, “Hinotama, Called Shot: Arm”.

 The attack has a -4 penalty to its CQC, Ninjutsu, or Weapon Accuracy. However, if it hits, any wounds it inflicts will automatically be dealt to that location. You can not use Called Shot with Area of Effect attacks, except for Targeted Area of Effect attacks, where you can make the attack a Called Shot against your chosen target, but anyone else within range to be hit by it will not consider it a Called Shot (though the penalty to its CQC, Ninjutsu, or Weapon Accuracy applies regardless, as it is penalizing the attack itself).

### **Equipment**

Every piece of equipment has a cost in Ryo, the world's most commonly-used currency. You can get Ryo in two primary ways, the Money: Requisition and Money: Savings abilities.

Items bought with Requisition can be traded in and replaced whenever you visit your village. Items bought with Savings are yours forever, for better or worse; if you lose them (or break them), they won't be replaced.

Items can be divided into two broad categories: Consumable and non-consumable. Consumable items are gone once you use them. These are things like smoke bombs, poisons, and explosives. Used-up consumables can be restocked when you get back to your village, if they were bought with Requisition. Non-consumable items can be broken, destroyed, or lost, but usually aren't. These are things like ropes, weapons, and lockpicks. Ranged weapons are not destroyed on use (hit or miss).

Items with the 'Armor' tag indicate that they are, as you'd expect, Armor. Some might have penalties for wearing them, but not all. Most importantly, you can only have one type of Armor protecting any given body location (which the armor itself will state where it applies to). You can, for RP purposes, wear multiple types over each other, but mechanically you must pick which you wish to use and consider to be wearing for its benefits. While you can remove armor in combat, you can't easily put it on, unless the Armor itself states otherwise.

### Equipment weight

In some cases, equipment weight will come up. Weights are listed below.

- Large Gourd: 66 lb (Full)
- Small Gourd: 16 lb (Full)
- Any melee weapon: 5 lb
- Any large ranged weapon: 3 lb
- Any other ranged weapon: Trivial
- Poisons: Trivial
- Basic equipment/supplies/unlisted: Trivial

## Weapon-Specific Actions

### Draw Weapon (Speed 3)

Weapons are typically carried attached to one's person somehow; swords have sheathes, a pair of tonfa might be tucked through belt holsters, a fan could be strapped across your back, and so forth. Drawing a weapon is retrieving it from wherever you have it stored and placing it in your hands. At this point you can use it to attack, parry, or whatever else you please, with the downside being that your hands aren't free for, say, handseals. You may also draw a weapon at the start of combat (when you roll initiative), without taking an action to do so. If you wish, you can draw two weapons as a single Speed 5 action. Note that you only need to Draw weapons you intend to hold and use in melee. This Speed to attack with thrown or multi-thrown weapons includes the time needed to retrieve them from holsters. The same is true for Bows.

Be aware: While holding one or more weapons, you can still make basic unarmed attacks. You cannot, however, use CQC Taijutsu. Except only Grappling is still possible as long as you have at least one hand free.

### Sheathe Weapon (Speed 6)

You put a weapon you're holding away, in some secure fashion. If you're holding two weapons, you can put them both away at the same time. You can only sheath weapons which you entered the scene with: any weapons you acquire in a scene (such as from disarming someone) must be held in hand and can not be 'stowed' in any way, unless they are small ranged thrown weapoons, or you have some special means to stow the weapon, such as Confinement Seal Scroll.

### Discard Weapon (Speed 1, or Speed +2)

You drop or toss aside one or more weapons you are holding in your hands. Done normally, this is a Speed 1 action. However, it may also be used alongside a defensive interrupt by increasing that action's Speed by 2.

### Retrieve Weapon (Speed 5)

You pick up a weapon on the ground. This can be your weapon, or someone else's, as long as nobody's currently holding it. You can only pick up one melee weapon at a time with this action, and must be able to physically reach it (which may require you to move first). You can also pick up DEX/10 used throwing weapons and holster them appropriately.

If two people decide to be stubborn and attempt to retrieve the same weapon on the same IC, they can either allow only one of them to pick it up, or both can pick up the weapon (in which case, they both pick up only the one contested weapon, even if it was a throwing weapon, and can not pick up others), at which point both are considered to be wielding the weapon, causing them both to take the effects of being in a grapple, as listed under 'Grab' (E-rank Grapple jutsu), except that other grapple jutsu may not be used (as you are not grappling them, but a weapon), and both must keep one hand on the weapon (rather than on the defender). Ignite damage is not transferred through this 'grapple', but all other rules apply. Both people holding the weapon can use it to attack the other person with, but neither can holster or throw it, and the weapon itself can not be used for the Disarm action (though it can be used to parry a disarm attempt).

### Disarm (Weapon's Speed +4)

You use a weapon you're holding to knock an enemy's out of their grasp, usually by hitting it at such an angle, and with enough force, that it becomes physically impossible for them to hold onto it. This is a basic weapon attack that does no damage, but if it hits you knock their weapon STR/10 yards in any direction (if straight up, the weapon lands at their feet but cannot be retrieved for STR/10 IC). Trying to Parry a Disarm attempt has a -3 penalty. Small weapons cannot disarm Large weapons, and Large weapons lack the finesse to be used to disarm at all.

# **Downtime**

Every half hour your character spends inside of a RP scene they generate one (1) downtime (1 per full hour in sparring scenes), which you log on your sheet in the Downtime tab. You can hold up to 10 Downtime at once, any more than that is simply lost.

## **Recovery**

Hitpoints, Vitality, Fatigue and Willpower are reset at the start of every scene or event. Wounds heal on their own timer (see Wound Recovery on the Wounds page) and cannot be recovered with Downtime.
